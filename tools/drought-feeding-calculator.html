Drought Feeding Calculator

Calculate how much supplemental feed your livestock need when pasture is insufficient during drought or dry season.

This calculator bridges the gap between required dry matter intake and available pasture. It computes daily supplement per head, total tonnage needed for the supplementation period, total cost, and cost per animal. Essential for drought contingency planning.

A 50-head beef herd in a dry season needs 12 kg DM/head/day but only finds 7 kg in pasture. That's 5 kg/day supplement × 50 head × 60 days = 15 tonnes of supplement at 90% DM. At $320/ton, total cost is ~$5,333 — $107 per head. Plan ahead to avoid panic buying at peak drought prices.

How to Use This Calculator

Select your animal type. Enter required daily dry matter, number of animals, available pasture DM per head, supplementation days, supplement price per ton, and supplement dry matter percentage. Click Calculate.

About This Tool

The Drought Feeding Calculator helps producers plan for periods of pasture shortage. NRC specifies dry matter requirements by production class. Proper drought planning prevents weight loss, reproductive failure, and emergency feed purchases at inflated prices.

Applicability: This calculator is designed for global livestock operations, with particular relevance for North American and European cattle, pig, and poultry farms.

How the Calculation Works

Daily supplement = required DM − available pasture DM (if positive). Total as-fed = daily supp DM × heads × days ÷ (supplement DM%). Cost = total tonnes × price/ton. For mature cows, NRC recommends 2.0-2.5% of body weight as dry matter daily.

Real-World Example

50 cows × 12 kg required − 7 kg available = 5 kg supp/day. Total DM = 5 × 50 × 60 = 15,000 kg DM. As-fed (90% DM) = 16.67 tonnes. Cost = 16.67 × $320 = $5,333. Per head = $106.67.

Common Mistakes & Tips

Common mistakes: not converting between as-fed and dry matter, underestimating drought duration, forgetting that pregnant/lactating animals need more. Tip: buy feed before drought peaks — prices can rise 30-50%. Test hay quality. NRC requirements increase by 10-20% in cold weather.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much supplement do cattle need during drought?

Supplement = required DM minus available pasture. For a 500 kg cow: 10-12 kg DM/day maintenance, 12-15 kg for lactating. If pasture provides 6 kg, supplement 4-9 kg. NRC specifies DM at 2.0-2.5% of BW for maintenance, rising to 3.0% for lactation. Adjust for cold weather (+10-20%).

How do I prepare my herd for drought?

Start drought planning 3-6 months before expected dry season. Key steps: (1) Calculate carrying capacity using forage yield data, (2) Develop a culling plan — sell low producers early, (3) Price-lock feed contracts before shortages, (4) Test hay quality for accurate supplementation, (5) Have alternative water sources. FAO recommends monitoring rainfall and pasture growth weekly.

What is the cheapest drought feed option?

Options ranked by cost: (1) Stockpiled forage/hay — cheapest if available, (2) Grain byproducts (distillers grains, wheat midds) — often $200-300/ton, (3) Hay/silage — $150-250/ton, (4) Commercial supplements — $300-500/ton. Buy in bulk before drought peaks. NRC recommends minimum 7-8% CP in diet for maintenance ruminants.

How long can cattle survive on low pasture?

Cattle can lose 10-15% body weight before serious health issues occur. At 50% of required intake, weight loss accelerates after 30-45 days. Pregnant cows in third trimester are most vulnerable — abortion risk increases. NRC recommends not letting body condition score drop below 4/9. Supplement before condition deteriorates.

When should I sell cattle during drought?

Best time to sell is BEFORE the drought peak when prices are higher and supply is better. Cull open cows, old cows, and low performers first. Keep your best breeding stock. Emergency selling at drought peak means 20-40% lower prices. FAO recommends developing a drought contingency plan with triggers: when pasture drops below 50% of normal, initiate culling plan.
