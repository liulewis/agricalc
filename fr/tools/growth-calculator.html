Map key crop growth stages and predict harvest date from planting date and total growing days. Covers emergence, seedling, vegetative, reproductive, grain fill, and maturity stages for corn, rice, wheat, soybean, and custom crops. Plan field operations aroundevelopment stages.

You side-dressed nitrogen on your corn when the plants were knee-high, thinking that was the right timing. But your variety was a fast-developing 90-day corn — by knee-high it was already at V8, and the critical N uptake period (V10-V14) was 10 days away. By the time the nitrogen became available, the crop had already passed peak demand. Yield was down 12%. Timing fertilizer to growth stage is more important than the amount.

Questions Fréquemment Posées

What is the optimal Growth for different livestock species?

Optimal Growth varies by species and stage: cattle growing calves gain 0.8-1.2 kg/day, mature cows maintain at 2% body weight DM/day; pigs grow 0.7-0.9 kg/day with FCR 2.5-3.0; poultry broilers reach 2 kg in 5-6 weeks with FCR 1.6-1.9; sheep lambs gain 0.2-0.3 kg/day. Genetics, nutrition, health, and environment all influence performance. Regular weighing and record-keeping identify underperforming animals. Consult NRC (National Research Council) publications for species-specific nutrient requirements at each production stage.

How do nutrition and Growth relate?

Nutrition is the primary driver of Growth. Energy fuels maintenance and growth; protein builds muscle and supports reproduction; minerals/vitamins are essential for bone, immune function, and metabolism. Deficiencies limit growth even with adequate energy. Cattle on low-protein forage (<7% CP) cannot digest fiber efficiently, reducing intake and gain. Ration balancing using forage analysis and NRC standards optimizes Growth while minimizing feed costs. For ruminants, forage quality determines intake potential — high-quality forage supports higher intake and gain.

What health issues affect Growth?

Health issues reducing Growth: internal parasites (reduce nutrient absorption 10-30%), respiratory disease (shipping fever costs $50-100+/animal), digestive disorders (acidosis on high-grain diets), lameness (reduces feed intake), reproductive failures, metabolic diseases (milk fever, ketosis). Prevention through vaccination, parasite control, proper nutrition, biosecurity, and stress reduction is far more cost-effective than treatment. Regular health monitoring and prompt veterinary intervention minimize production losses. A good health program returns $3-5 for every $1 invested.

How do I measure and track Growth?

Measure Growth by: regular weighing (cattle every 30-60 days, pigs every 2-4 weeks), using calibrated scales or weight tapes (cattle heart girth estimates weight within 5-10%), recording group/individual weights, calculating ADG = (final − initial) ÷ days, tracking feed intake for FCR = feed ÷ gain, maintaining records in spreadsheets or herd software. Compare actual performance against breed standards and historical data to identify problems early. For pasture cattle, use periodic weight checks to evaluate forage quality and stocking rate.

What environmental factors influence Growth?

Environmental factors: temperature (heat stress above 25-30°C reduces feed intake 10-30%; cold stress increases energy needs), humidity (exacerbates heat stress), ventilation (poor air quality causes respiratory disease), stocking density (overcrowding increases stress, aggression, disease), lighting (proper cycles improve poultry growth/reproduction), water quality (clean water essential — intake correlates with feed intake). THI >72 indicates mild heat stress in cattle, >78 severe. Provide shade, fans, sprinklers, windbreaks, and clean water to maximize genetic potential for growth.
