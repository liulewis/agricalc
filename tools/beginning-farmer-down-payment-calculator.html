Beginning Farmer Down Payment Calculator

Plan a farmland purchase under the FSA Down Payment Loan for beginning farmers: see the 5% cash down payment, the FSA loan, and the commercial lender share side by side.

Under the official FSA 5-45-50 structure, the borrower puts down 5% cash, FSA finances up to 45% of the purchase price (maximum loan $300,150, 20-year term, reduced interest rate), and a commercial lender or seller covers the rest with at least a 30-year amortization and no balloon payment in the first 20 years. Enter the purchase price, appraised value, and commercial lender terms to get both monthly payments.

The remaining $459,850 goes to a commercial lender. At the October 2026 FSA rate of 2.250% over 20 years, the FSA monthly payment is about $1,554. About This Tool

The Beginning Farmer Down Payment Calculator helps beginning farmers and ranchers plan a farmland purchase under the FSA Down Payment Loan program. It splits the purchase into the 5% cash down payment, the FSA loan (up to 45% of the purchase price, capped at $300,150, 20-year term at the reduced rate), and the remaining share financed by a commercial lender or the seller.

Applicability: U.S. farmland purchases by eligible beginning farmers and ranchers (generally operating a farm for 10 years or less).

How the Calculation Works

  • Down payment = purchase price × 5%
  • FSA loan = min(45% × min(purchase price, appraised value, $667,000), $300,150); 20-year term
  • FSA rate = max(direct ownership rate − 4 percentage points, 1.5%); October 2026 preset: 6.250% − 4 pp = 2.250%
  • Monthly payment (equal-payment amortization) = P × r × (1+r)^n ÷ ((1+r)^n − 1), with r = annual rate ÷ 12 and n = total months
  • Commercial/seller share = purchase price − down payment − FSA loan; minimum 30-year amortization, no balloon payment due during the first 20 years

Real-World Example

A beginning farmer buys farmland for $800,000; the appraised value is $780,000.

Down payment: $800,000 × 5% = $40,000

FSA loan: min(45% × min($800,000, $780,000, $667,000), $300,150) = min(45% × $667,000 = $300,150, $300,150) = $300,150 — the cap binds, so the calculator shows the cap notice.

FSA rate: 6.250% − 4 percentage points = 2.250% (October 2026 preset, above the 1.5% floor). Over 20 years (240 months): $300,150 × (0.0225/12) × (1+0.0225/12)^240 ÷ ((1+0.0225/12)^240 − 1) = $1,554.20/month.

Commercial share: $800,000 − $40,000 − $300,150 = $459,850, financed by a commercial lender or seller with at least a 30-year amortization and no balloon payment in the first 20 years.

Common Mistakes & Tips

Assuming FSA always covers 45% (the $300,150 cap binds on purchases above about $667,000); ignoring a low appraisal (FSA’s share uses the lower of price and appraised value); treating the 2.250% preset as current (FSA updates rates monthly — verify the latest rate); expecting a shorter commercial term (program rules require at least 30-year amortization with no balloon in the first 20 years); forgetting the 5% cash down payment must come from the borrower. Tip: if the cap binds on your numbers, ask your lender to quote a 30-year schedule on the larger commercial share — that is where the monthly total moves.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who qualifies as a beginning farmer or rancher?

FSA generally defines a beginning farmer or rancher as someone who has operated a farm or ranch for 10 years or less (requirements vary slightly by program; some use crop years). For the Down Payment Loan you must also meet FSA eligibility rules, complete borrower training, and provide the 5% cash down payment from your own resources. Confirm eligibility with your local FSA office before counting on the program.

What does the 5-45-50 structure mean?

It describes how the purchase is financed: 5% is the borrower’s cash down payment, up to 45% is financed by FSA as a Down Payment Loan (maximum $300,150, 20-year term, reduced rate), and the remaining share — roughly 50%, more when the FSA cap binds — is financed by a commercial lender or the seller with at least a 30-year amortization and no balloon payment in the first 20 years.

How is the FSA Down Payment Loan interest rate set?

The rate equals the direct farm ownership loan rate minus 4 percentage points, with a floor of 1.5%. Using the October 2026 direct ownership rate of 6.250%, the down payment loan rate is 2.250%. FSA updates farm loan rates monthly; this calculator presets the October 2026 rate and lets you override it.

How is the loan calculated when the appraised value is below the purchase price?

FSA bases its 45% share on the lower of the purchase price, the appraised value, or $667,000, then applies the $300,150 cap. If you overpay relative to the appraisal, the FSA share shrinks and your down payment plus commercial loan must cover the difference.

What term requirements apply to the commercial lender portion?

The commercial lender or seller must amortize their share over at least 30 years, with no balloon payment due during the first 20 years. This protects the borrower from a large lump-sum payment while the FSA loan is still being repaid.
