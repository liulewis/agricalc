USDA's REAP Final Rule: What the New Solar Restrictions Mean for Your Farm

7 Oct 2026 Agricalc Editorial Team 7 min read

Related Calculators: agri subsidy calculator · farm ROI & payback calculator · equipment cost calculator

Introduction

USDA published a final rule last week that could end federal support for most solar projects on farms. Under the changes to the Rural Energy for America Program (REAP), farmers could no longer apply for a grant until 12 months after a project is fully built, ground-mount solar on certified cropland is prohibited, and systems using Chinese-made components are disqualified. Farm and environmental groups are already suing to stop the changes. Here is what changed, the timeline that led here, and what it means for your project math.

The Three New Restrictions

According to the final rule as reported on October 5, 2026, the key changes are:

ChangeWhat it means in practice
12-month wait to applyYou cannot apply for REAP funding until the project has been fully built and operating for 12 months — you must finance and build the system with no guarantee of reimbursement.
No ground-mount solar on certified croplandGround-mounted arrays on certified cropland lose eligibility entirely.
No Chinese-made componentsSystems using materials manufactured in China are disqualified — China manufactures the vast majority of the world's solar panels.

The 12-month rule is the most consequential for project planning: it converts the grant from project financing into a retroactive reimbursement you may never receive. USDA says the changes "restore integrity and stability" to the program; the challengers say they violate Congress's directive to "promote energy efficiency and renewable energy development for agricultural producers and rural small businesses."

Timeline: How We Got Here

  • August 2025 — Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins begins limiting REAP funding, characterizing solar development as a threat to farmland. (Context: more than 40% of new solar and over half of new wind turbines were installed on former cropland between 2012 and 2020 — but that land equals less than 0.05% of U.S. farmland.)
  • March 2026 — USDA pauses processing of all REAP grants and disbursements. Some farmers who had already applied, been approved, and invested in building solar have not received promised reimbursements.
  • Late September / early October 2026 — Earthjustice and the Environmental Law and Policy Center, on behalf of the Iowa Farmers Union, solar developers, and others, file a lawsuit challenging the gutting of REAP.
  • Week of September 28, 2026 — USDA publishes the final rule codifying the restrictions.
  • November 2, 2026 — Public comment period on the final rule closes.

Over the past decade, tens of thousands of farms used REAP loans and grants to build renewable energy systems. That pipeline is now effectively frozen for new solar applicants.

What This Means for Your Project Math

First, treat any REAP grant as zero in your base-case cash flow. With a 12-month post-build wait and eligibility screens on land type and component origin, the grant is a possible upside, not a funding source. Run your payback through our farm ROI & payback calculator with the full system cost out of pocket and the grant as a bonus scenario.

Second, check your siting and bill of materials before spending. If the array would sit on certified cropland, or your panels trace to Chinese manufacturing, the project is ineligible under the final rule as written — no amount of paperwork fixes that.

Third, watch the lawsuit and the comment window. The rule is being challenged in court, and comments are open until November 2, 2026. Rules under litigation can change; do not sign long-lead equipment contracts on the assumption that the final rule is the last word.

Note: our agri subsidy calculator covers commodity, conservation, crop-insurance, and equipment programs — it does not model REAP energy grants. Use the ROI calculator above for solar project economics under the new rules.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

First, budgeting as if the grant is approved funding — under the final rule you build first and apply a year later, with no guarantee. Second, assuming rooftop or non-cropland sites are automatically safe — the reported summary of the rule centers on ground-mount systems on certified cropland and component origin; the full rule text should be checked for your specific configuration before committing capital. Third, ignoring the comment deadline: November 2, 2026 is the last day to get concerns on the record.

Conclusion

The REAP final rule does not just trim a program — it restructures farm solar economics. Build first, wait a year, apply, and hope: that is the new sequence, and it screens out ground-mount projects on certified cropland and most panel supply chains. Plan your project as if the grant does not exist, keep an eye on the lawsuit, and file a comment before November 2 if the rule affects you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I still apply for a REAP grant if my solar project is already built?

Under the final rule, yes — but only after the project has been fully built and operating for 12 months. You must also clear the other screens: no ground-mount array on certified cropland, and no Chinese-made components. Farmers whose projects were approved under the old rules but never got paid after the March 2026 pause are part of the group challenging the changes in court.

Does the 12-month wait apply to wind turbines too?

The reported final rule covers REAP renewable-energy projects generally — the program historically funded both solar arrays and wind turbines. Check the full rule text for your project type before planning around it.

Are rooftop solar systems affected?

The reported summary of the rule targets ground-mount solar on certified cropland; rooftop installations are not named in that summary. This is an unverified detail — read the full rule text or consult your state rural development office before committing capital.

What counts as a Chinese-made component?

The rule prohibits systems using materials made in China, which manufactures the vast majority of the world's solar panels. The exact scope — panels only, or also inverters, racking, and subcomponents — is in the full rule text and should be verified with the official document.

When is the deadline to comment on the final rule?

November 2, 2026. USDA is accepting public comments on the final rule through that date.

Could the lawsuit reverse these changes?

Possibly. Earthjustice and the Environmental Law and Policy Center, representing the Iowa Farmers Union, solar developers, and others, argue the changes violate Congress's directive to promote renewable energy for agricultural producers and rural small businesses. Litigation outcomes and timelines are uncertain — do not sign long-lead contracts assuming a reversal.
