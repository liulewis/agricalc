Cattle Feed Ration Calculator

Calculate a basic cattle ration: daily dry matter intake, forage and concentrate amounts, crude protein level, and daily feed cost by production stage.

This calculator estimates daily dry matter intake based on body weight and production stage, splits it into forage and concentrate portions, checks crude protein adequacy, and computes daily ration cost. Based on NRC recommendations for beef and dairy cattle.

A 500 kg growing steer (2.5% DMI) needs 12.5 kg DM/day at a 60:40 forage-to-concentrate ratio. That's 7.5 kg corn silage + 5.0 kg corn grain. Ration CP = (7.5×8% + 5.0×9%) ÷ 12.5 = 8.4% — below the 12% growing requirement. Add 1.5 kg soybean meal or switch to alfalfa to fix the deficiency. Cost = (7.5×$150 + 6.5×$350) ÷ 1000 = $3.40/day.

How to Use This Calculator

Enter cow weight in kg, select production stage, forage type, concentrate type, target daily gain, forage price, and concentrate price. Click Calculate.

About This Tool

The Cattle Feed Ration Calculator provides a starting point for ration formulation. NRC (Nutrient Requirements of Beef/Dairy Cattle) specifies DMI as 2.0-3.5% of body weight depending on production stage. This tool checks crude protein adequacy but doesn't replace full ration balancing with lab-tested forage analysis.

Applicability: This calculator is designed for global livestock operations, with particular relevance for North American and European cattle, pig, and poultry farms.

How the Calculation Works

DMI = BW × DMI% (maintenance 2.0%, growing 2.5%, finishing 3.0%, lactating 3.5%). Forage:concentrate ratio by stage: 80:20 maintenance, 60:40 growing, 45:55 finishing, 50:50 lactating. CP = (forage kg × forage CP% + conc kg × conc CP%) ÷ total DMI. Cost = (forage tonne × price) + (conc tonne × price).

Real-World Example

BW 500 kg, growing (2.5%): DMI = 12.5 kg. Forage 60% = 7.5 kg corn silage (8% CP), conc 40% = 5.0 kg corn (9% CP). CP = (7.5×0.08 + 5.0×0.09) ÷ 12.5 = (0.6+0.45) ÷ 12.5 = 8.4%. Need 12% for growing — deficient. Cost = 0.0075×$150 + 0.005×$350 = $1.125 + $1.75 = $2.88/day.

Common Mistakes & Tips

Common mistakes: using body weight not adjusted for frame condition, assuming all corn silage has same CP, not accounting for day-to-day variation. Tip: for precise rations, test forage by lab. This calculator is a planning tool, not a complete ration balancer. NRC recommends minimum CP: maintenance 8%, growing 12%, finishing 12%, lactating 16%.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the rule of thumb for cattle DMI?

Dry matter intake is typically 2.0-3.5% of body weight: maintenance 2.0%, growing 2.5%, finishing 3.0%, lactating dairy 3.5-4.0%. For a 500 kg cow: 10-17.5 kg DM/day. NRC refines this based on milk production, age, frame size, and environmental temperature. Hot weather reduces intake 10-30%; cold weather increases it.

What is a good forage-to-concentrate ratio?

By production stage: maintenance 80:20 (all forage), growing 60:40, finishing 45:55 (high grain for marbling), lactating dairy 50:50 or 40:60. Ruminants need adequate forage (minimum 40-50% of ration) to maintain rumen health. Below 40% forage increases risk of acidosis, bloat, and laminitis. NRC recommends minimum effective fiber.

How much crude protein do cattle need?

NRC requirements: mature maintenance 7-8% CP, growing calves 12-14%, finishing cattle 11-12%, lactating cows 16-18%. Deficiency limits growth and milk production. Excess CP is excreted as urea (wasteful and polluting). Test forage CP by lab. This calculator flags if CP is below stage requirements.

How much does a cattle ration cost per day?

Maintenance cow: $2-4/day (mostly forage). Growing stocker: $3-5/day. Finishing feedlot: $5-8/day (high grain). Lactating dairy: $8-15/day (high-energy ration). Feed cost is 60-75% of total production cost. Reducing ration cost by 10% through better forage and least-cost formulation typically improves margin 5-10%.

How do I balance a cattle ration properly?

Steps: (1) Test forage by lab (CP, TDN, NDF, ADF, minerals), (2) Determine animal nutrient requirements (NRC), (3) Calculate what forage provides, (4) Determine supplement needed to meet gaps, (5) Use least-cost formulation software or feed tag, (6) Monitor animal performance and adjust. This calculator provides a starting estimate. Full ration balancing should use professional software or a nutritionist.
