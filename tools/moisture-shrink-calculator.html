Formula & How It Works

Shrink % = [(Initial Moisture% − Final Moisture%) ÷ (100 − Final Moisture%)] × 100

This formula accounts for the fact that dry matter is conserved — only water is removed. The denominator (100 − final moisture) converts to dry matter basis.

Final Weight = Initial Weight × (1 − Shrink% ÷ 100)

Weight Loss = Initial Weight − Final Weight

Bushels = Weight (lb) ÷ Test Weight (lb/bu)

Calculation Example

50,000 lb of corn at 25% moisture dried to 15%: Shrink = [(25 − 15) ÷ (100 − 15)] × 100 = (10 ÷ 85) × 100 = 11.76%.

Final weight = 50,000 × (1 − 0.1176) = 44,120 lb. Weight loss = 5,880 lb. At 56 lb/bu: Initial = 893 bu, Final = 788 bu. At $4.50/bu: Revenue loss = 105 bu × $4.50 = $472.

About This Tool

Moisture shrink is a critical factor in grain marketing and drying economics. When grain is dried, only water is removed — dry matter (and the nutrients it contains) is conserved. The shrink formula correctly accounts for this by using dry matter basis in the denominator. Understanding shrink helps you compare the cost of on-farm drying versus selling wet grain at a discount, and evaluate whether drying revenue covers drying costs (fuel, electricity, labor).

Applicability: Relevant for grain storage operations in North America and Europe.

Common Mistakes & Tips

Common mistakes: Using simple subtraction (initial − final) instead of the correct formula — this underestimates shrink because it doesn't account for dry matter conservation. Forgetting that test weight can change during drying — dried grain may have higher test weight. Confusing moisture shrink with handling loss — shrink is water removal only, not physical loss from spills or augers. Ignoring that different commodities have different target moistures.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why can't I just subtract final moisture from initial moisture? ▼
Simple subtraction gives the percentage points of moisture removed, but shrink must account for the fact that the final weight is smaller. The correct formula divides by (100 − final moisture) to express the loss relative to the final dry matter weight. For 25%→15%, simple subtraction gives 10% but actual shrink is 11.76%.
What are standard target moistures for storage? ▼
Corn: 15% for short-term storage, 14% for long-term (6+ months). Soybeans: 13%. Wheat: 13.5%. Sorghum: 14%. Storing grain above these levels risks spoilage, mold, and insect infestation.
How do I decide whether to dry or sell wet? ▼
Compare the drying cost per bushel (fuel + electricity + labor + equipment) with the moisture discount the elevator charges for wet grain. If drying cost is less than the discount, drying is profitable. Also consider storage time and quality preservation.
What units does the Moisture Shrink Calculator use? ▼
Moisture in %, initial weight in lb, sale price in $/bu, test weight in lb/bu. Results in shrink %, final weight, bushels, and revenue impact.
How do I use the Moisture Shrink Calculator correctly? ▼
Start by gathering accurate input values from your farm records or measurements. Enter each value in the specified units, then click Calculate. Review all results carefully — cross-check the primary output against your own experience or extension recommendations. For best results, use actual measured values rather than estimates, and recalibrate whenever conditions change.
