Grain Dockage Calculator

Quantify the weight and value lost to foreign material in your grain.

Enter total load weight, measured dockage (foreign material) percentage, the elevator's deduction rate, and test weight. The calculator gives dockage weight, net saleable weight, dockage bushels, and the economic loss.

How the Calculation Works

Dockage weight = total weight × dockage % ÷ 100. Net weight = total − dockage. Dockage bushels = dockage weight ÷ test weight. Economic loss = dockage bushels × deduction rate per bushel.

Real-World Example

50,000 lb at 3% dockage: dockage = 1,500 lb, net = 48,500 lb. At 56 lb/bu: 26.8 bu of dockage. At $0.10/bu deduction: $2.68 loss per load — small per load but adds up over a season.

Common Mistakes & Tips

Common mistakes: guessing dockage instead of sieving a sample; confusing dockage (foreign material) with moisture; applying the deduction to gross instead of net weight; not keeping a copy of the probe report.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is dockage?

Dockage is foreign material — chaff, broken kernels, weed seeds, dirt, stems — mixed with the grain. Elevators remove and weigh it out before paying you for clean grain.

How is dockage measured?

A probe sample is sieved and separated; the foreign material is weighed as a percentage of the sample. Standard dockage is usually 1–2% for clean grain; 3%+ triggers discounts.

What raises dockage?

Weed seed pressure, combining too fast, open concave settings, worn screens, harvest weeds before they shatter, and grain comingles with chaff. Adjust combine settings and sample loads.

Is dockage the same as moisture?

No. Dockage is physical foreign material; moisture is water in the grain. Both can reduce what you are paid, but they are deducted by different mechanisms.

Can I challenge a dockage charge?

Yes. Keep a duplicate probe sample sealed and dated. If you disagree, request a re-check or use a licensed grader. Some states have grain inspection dispute processes.
