Planting & Crop Management

Plants per Acre: A Complete Guide by Crop

Plants per acre = 43,560 ÷ (in-row spacing in feet × between-row spacing in feet). For tomatoes at 18 in × 48 in: 43,560 ÷ (1.5 × 4) = 7,260 plants/acre. For corn at 8 in × 30 in: 43,560 ÷ (0.67 × 2.5) = 26,136 plants/acre. Use our calculators to get exact numbers for your spacing and field size.

Agricalc Editorial Team 6 min read

Why Plant Population Matters

Plant population is the foundation of yield. Too dense: plants compete for light, water, and nutrients; increased disease pressure; smaller ears/fruit; lodging (falling over). Too sparse: wasted sunlight and land; lower total yield; more weeds. The optimal population balances individual plant performance with total area utilization. Modern corn hybrids achieve maximum yield at 30,000-36,000 plants/acre — 50% higher than 1970s hybrids, because modern varieties tolerate higher density without barrenness.

How to Do a Stand Count

A stand count tells your actual plant population vs. target. Method: 1) Measure 1/1000 acre length based on rowidth. For 30-inch rows: 17 ft 5 in (1/1000 acre). For 15-inch rows: 34 ft 10 in. 2) Count plants in that length in 5-10 representative locations. 3) Average the counts and multiply by 1000 = plants/acre. 4) Compare to target. If below 90% of target, consider replanting (for corn) or accepting lower yield (for soybeans, which can compensate with branching). Stand counts are most accurate at V2-V4 (2-4 leaf stage) for corn and after full emergence for all crops.

Factors That Change Optimal Spacing

Soil Fertility and Water

High fertility + irrigation = can pushigher populations (more plants can be supported). Low fertility + dryland = reduce population by 10-20% (fewer plants share limited resources). In dry years, over-populated fields suffer more because each plant has access to less water. In irrigated, high-fertility fields, the yield-limiting factor is usually light interception, so higher populations are beneficial.

Maturity and Growing Season

Early-maturing varieties = smaller plants = can plant denser. Late-maturing varieties = larger plants = need wider spacing. Short growing season (northern latitudes, high elevation) = use earlier varieties and higher populations to maximize light interception in limited time. Long growing season = later varieties with wider spacing, allowing individual plants to reach full potential.

Equipment and Management

Row spacing is often determined by equipment (30-inch rows for corn headers, 15-inch for soybean drills). Narrower rows (15-20 inch corn) can increase yield by 3-8% through faster canopy closure and less weed pressure, but require specialized equipment. Bed planting (vegetables) allows precise spacing and drip irrigation. Transplanting vs direct seeding: transplants allow more precise spacing and earlier maturity, but cost more per plant.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate plants per acre from row and plant spacing?

Plants per acre = 43,560 (sq ft/acre) ÷ (Row spacing in feet × Plant spacing in feet). Example: Tomatoes at 5-foot rows × 2-foot plant spacing = 43,560 ÷ (5 × 2) = 4,356 plants/acre. For inches: convert to feet first (divide by 12). Corn at 30-inch rows (2.5 ft) × 8-inch spacing (0.667 ft) = 43,560 ÷ (2.5 × 0.667) = 26,136 plants/acre. A plant population calculator handles any spacing combination and accounts for germination rate and stand loss to determine actual seeding rate needed.

What is the optimal plant population for common crops?

Optimal populations vary by crop, variety, soil, and climate. Typical final stands: Corn (grain): 28,000–36,000 plants/acre (irrigated/high-yield: 34,000–40,000; dryland: 20,000–28,000); Soybeans: 100,000–140,000 plants/acre; Wheat: 1.0–1.5 million plants/acre; Cotton: 30,000–50,000 plants/acre; Tomatoes: 3,500–5,000 plants/acre; Lettuce (head): 10,000–18,000 plants/acre; Carrots: 250,000–400,000 plants/acre; Potatoes: 16,000–22,000 plants/acre; Apples (dwarf): 400–700 trees/acre; Blueberries: 1,000–1,500 plants/acre. Use a plants per acre calculator to match population to your specific conditions and yield goals.

How does plant population affect crop yield?

Yield response to plant population follows a curve: too low = wasted resources and weeds fill gaps; too high = interplant competition, lodging, smaller ears/fruits, and disease. The optimal population maximizes yield per acre while maintaining individual plant health. For corn, each 1,000 plants/acre increase (up to optimum) adds ~3–5 bu/acre, but above optimum yields plateau or decline. For soybeans, yields plateau above 100,000 plants/acre — higher populations don't increase yield but increase seed cost. For vegetables, optimal spacing maximizes marketable yield (size + count) — overcrowding produces smaller, less valuable produce. A seed spacing calculator helps find the sweet spot for your crop and market.

How do I calculate seed rate from desired plant population?

Seed rate (seeds/acre) = Desired plant population ÷ (Germination % ÷ 100 × Expected stand %). Expected stand is typically 80–95% of germinated seed (some seeds fail to emerge due to soil conditions, insects, disease). Example: Target 30,000 corn plants/acre, 95% germination, 90% expected stand: 30,000 ÷ (0.95 × 0.90) = 30,000 ÷ 0.855 = 35,088 seeds/acre. At 80,000 seeds per bag: 35,088 ÷ 80,000 = 0.44 bags/acre. A seed calculator accounts for seed size (seeds/lb), germination, and expected stand to give precise seeding rates and cost estimates.

What is the difference between in-row spacing and between-row spacing?

Between-row spacing (row width) is the distance between adjacent crop rows — determined by equipment width, cultivation needs, and sunlight penetration. Common row widths: 30 inches (corn, soybeans, cotton), 15 inches (soybeans, wheat), 36–42 inches (vegetables, sweet corn), 6–12 inches (small grains, solid-seeded). In-row spacing (plant spacing) is the distance between individual plants within a row — determined by desired population and crop size. Both factors multiply to determine plants per acre. Narrower rows (15 vs. 30 inches) can increase yield by 5–10% for crops that benefit from more equidistant spacing (soybeans, corn), but require specialized equipment. A plant population calculator lets you compare different row width × spacing combinations.

How do I convert plants per acre to plants per square foot or hectare?

Plants per square foot = Plants per acre ÷ 43,560. Example: 4,356 tomato plants/acre ÷ 43,560 = 0.1 plants/sq ft = 1 plant per 10 sq ft. Plants per hectare = Plants per acre × 2.471. Example: 30,000 corn plants/acre × 2.471 = 74,130 plants/hectare. Row spacing conversion: 30 inches = 76.2 cm = 0.762 meters. For metric: Plants per hectare = 10,000 (sq m/ha) ÷ (Row spacing in meters × Plant spacing in meters). Example: 0.762 m rows × 0.20 m spacing = 10,000 ÷ (0.762 × 0.20) = 65,617 plants/ha. A plants per acre calculator handles both imperial and metric units automatically.
