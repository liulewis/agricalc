How the Calculation Works

Deficit = standard test weight − actual test weight (lb/bu). Discount % = deficit ÷ standard test weight. Bushels deducted = delivered bushels × discount %. Dollar deduction = bushels deducted × deduction rate per bushel. Typical standards: corn No. 1 = 56 lb/bu, No. 2 = 54, No. 3 = 52.

Real-World Example

53 lb/bu actual vs 56 standard on 5,000 bu: deficit = 3 lb/bu, discount = 5.36%, bushels deducted = 268. At $0.30/bu: $80.40 deduction.

Common Mistakes & Tips

Common mistakes: comparing to the wrong standard (commodity-specific); using a flat 0.5 lb step when the proportional formula is more accurate; forgetting that low test weight also correlates with high moisture; not re-testing after drying.

Frequently Asked Questions

What test weight makes No. 2 corn?

USDA No. 2 corn requires 54 lb/bu minimum; No. 1 is 56 lb/bu. Each 2 lb drop is a grade. Below 52 lb/bu grain is No. 3 or lower and faces steep discounts.

Does drying improve test weight?

Often, yes. As grain dries it settles and kernels pack more densely, raising test weight by 0.5–2 lb/bu. Re-test after drying before selling.

Why is low test weight penalized?

Lower test weight means less grain density per volume. Elevators sell by weight, so a bushel that weighs less costs them storage, transport and handling. They pass that cost on via discounts.

What causes low test weight?

Drought stress, frost before maturity, high nitrogen with low stand, thin kernel filling, and harvesting too early. It is a quality signal, not just a number.

Should I wait for better test weight?

Compare storage costs and market price with the discount. Sometimes paying the discount and selling now beats storing, drying, and risking further quality loss.
