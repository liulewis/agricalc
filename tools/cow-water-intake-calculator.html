Dairy Cow Water Intake Calculator

Water is the cheapest nutrient in a dairy ration and the fastest to hurt you: a cow that drinks too little drops milk within hours. This calculator estimates how much a single cow actually drinks per day from her milk yield, dry matter intake, body weight and barn temperature, then scales it to your whole herd so you can size troughs, drinking spaces and water supply with confidence.

What it answers: Litres per cow per day, herd daily total, peak hourly demand, recommended trough capacity and the number of drinking spaces needed — plus the extra load heat puts on the system.

Worked example: A 650 kg cow producing 32 L/day at 22 °C eats about 21.8 kg of dry matter and drinks close to 100 L/day. A 120-cow herd therefore needs about 12 m³/day, peaks near 1.8 m³/hour, and should have at least 10 drinking spaces.

How to Use This Calculator

Pick the cow type, enter the number of cows, body weight and milk yield. Leave dry matter intake at 0 to have it estimated, or type in your own ration value if you know it. Enter the barn temperature — this is what drives the heat adjustment — and click Calculate.

About This Tool

Milk is roughly 87% water, so a cow's drinking tracks her lactation curve far more closely than her weight. A dry cow drinks 30–50 L/day; a high-producing cow drinks 80–130 L/day and considerably more in hot weather. Under-supply shows up fast as lower milk, lower feed intake and, in severe heat, real welfare problems.

How the Calculation Works

For a lactating cow: water (L/day) = 15.99 + 1.58 × DMI + 0.90 × milk (kg/day) + 1.20 × (temperature − 5 °C, counted only above 5 °C). For a dry cow: water (L/day) = 14.0 + 1.35 × DMI + 0.90 × (temperature − 5 °C). If dry matter intake is left at 0 it is estimated as 0.0185 × body weight + 0.305 × milk yield for lactating cows, or 0.0185 × body weight for dry cows. Peak hourly demand is taken as 15% of the daily total, and trough capacity as two hours of peak demand.

Worked Example

120 lactating cows, 650 kg, 32 L/day, barn at 22 °C. Estimated DMI = 0.0185 × 650 + 0.305 × 32 = 21.8 kg. Water per cow = 15.99 + 1.58 × 21.8 + 0.90 × 32 + 1.20 × 17 = 99.9 L/day. Herd total = 11,988 L/day. Peak hour = 1,798 L. Trough capacity = 3,596 L. Drinking spaces = 120 ÷ 12 = 10.

Practical Tips

  • Allow about 10 cm of linear trough space per cow and one drinking space per 10–15 head.
  • A cow can drink 20 L in under two minutes — refill rate limits intake just as much as trough volume.
  • Above about 20 °C intake climbs roughly 1.2 L per additional °C; above 30 °C it can jump 30–50%.
  • Cows prefer water between 15 and 27 °C and will drink less of near-freezing water in winter.
  • Clean troughs weekly — algae and feed debris cut intake long before capacity becomes the problem.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much water does a dairy cow drink per day?

A dry cow drinks 30–50 L/day. A high-producing lactating cow drinks 80–130 L/day, and more in hot weather. Because milk is about 87% water, intake follows milk yield closely.

Why does temperature change intake so much?

Cows cool themselves by panting and sweating. Above roughly 20 °C, water intake rises about 1.2 L for every extra °C; above 30 °C it can rise 30–50% as the cow works to shed heat.

How many drinking spaces does a herd need?

Plan one space per 10–15 cows, or about 10 cm of trough edge per cow, so the whole herd can drink within an hour of leaving the parlour.

Can I use this for beef cattle?

The equations are calibrated for dairy cattle. For beef herds, beef calves or mixed stock use the Water Trough Calculator, which works from per-animal base values by class.
