Drip Irrigation System Design for Small Farms: A Step-by-Step Guide

7 Sep 2026 AT 3 min read

Drip irrigation is the most water-efficient method available to small-scale farmers, delivering 90-95% of applied water directly to plant root zones compared to 60-70% for sprinklers and 40-50% for flood irrigation. For a 2-acre vegetable farm, that can mean saving over 500,000 gallons of water per growing season while increasing yields by 20-40%. This guide walks you through designing a complete drip system from scratch.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I design a drip irrigation system for a small farm?

Follow these steps: 1) Measure your field and map crop rows. 2) Determine your water source capacity and available pressure. 3) Calculate crop water requirements using a drip irrigation water requirement calculator per hectare. 4) Design zones based on crop type, soil, and water availability. 5) Size mainlines and sub-mains using friction loss formulas. 6) Select drip tape/tubing with appropriate emitter spacing and flow rate. 7) Add filtration, pressure regulation, and controls.

What emitter spacing should I use for different crops?

Emitter spacing depends on crop type and soil texture: For row crops (tomatoes, peppers) use 30-50 cm spacing on sandy soil, 50-75 cm on loam. For vine crops use 60-90 cm. For trees use 2-4 emitters per tree, spaced around the dripline. For close-spaced vegetables (lettuce, carrots) use inline drip tape with 20-30 cm emitter spacing. Sandy soils need closer spacing; clay soils can use wider spacing.

How do I size pipes for a drip irrigation system?

Pipe sizing is based on flow rate and acceptable friction loss (typically 5% of operating pressure per 100m). Use the Hazen-Williams or Darcy-Weisbach equation. As a rule of thumb: 16mm PE pipe handles up to 1.5 m³/h, 20mm up to 2.5 m³/h, 25mm up to 4 m³/h, 32mm up to 7 m³/h, 40mm up to 11 m³/h, 50mm up to 18 m³/h. Always size mainlines for total system flow and sub-mains for zone flow.

What size water tank do I need for drip irrigation?

Tank size = Daily Water Requirement × Number of Storage Days (typically 1-3 days). Calculate daily requirement using a water tank size calculator for drip irrigation: Daily Volume = Crop ETc (mm/day) × Area (m²) ÷ Irrigation Efficiency. For example, a 1-hectare tomato crop needing 6mm/day requires 60,000 liters/day. A 2-day storage tank would be 120,000 liters (120 m³).
