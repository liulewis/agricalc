How the Calculation Works

Total planting cost = Seed + Fuel + Labor + Other. Cost per unit area = Total cost ÷ Field area. The per-acre figure is derived from the per-hectare value (1 ha = 2.471 acres). Include all cash and operator costs to get a true picture.

Real-World Example

For a 50 ha field: seed $1,200 + fuel $350 + labor $400 + other $150 = $2,100 total. Cost per hectare = 2,100 ÷ 50 = $42/ha = $17/acre.

Common Mistakes & Tips

Common mistakes: forgetting fuel, lubrication and equipment ownership; excluding operator labor; using only seed cost; mixing units (acres vs hectares). Always separate cash costs from overhead and verify the area unit matches the per-unit output.

Frequently Asked Questions

What counts as a planting cost?

Planting costs include seed, seed treatment, fuel and lubrication for the tractor and planter, labor (hired and operator), custom hire, repairs, equipment depreciation allocated to planting, and any other direct cost incurred to place the crop in the ground.

Should I include my own labor?

Yes. Even if you do not write a paycheck, your labor has opportunity cost. Multiply your hours by a reasonable hourly wage or use the custom rate for the operation. Excluding operator labor understates true cost and distorts break-even analysis.

How do I compare fields of different sizes?

Use the cost per hectare or per acre figure, not the total. Two fields can have the same per-unit cost but very different totals. Per-unit cost lets you compare seed hybrids, tillage systems, and custom-vs-own equipment decisions.

Does this include dryland vs irrigated differences?

The calculator sums whatever costs you enter. Irrigated planting adds irrigation rig moves and water costs; dryland may add more tillage. Enter the line items that apply to your operation and the per-area figure will reflect them.

How accurate is this budget?

It is as accurate as your inputs. Use actual invoices for seed and fuel, record labor hours, and update quarterly. Extension crop budgets provide benchmark per-acre costs you can compare against to catch errors.
