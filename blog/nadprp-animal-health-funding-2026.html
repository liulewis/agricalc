USDA's NADPRP Animal Health Funding: $80 Million Explained for Livestock Producers

7 Nov 2026 Agricalc Editorial Team 6 min read

Related Calculators: Livestock Weight Estimator · Cattle Feed Ration Calculator · Veterinary Dosage Calculator

On November 5, 2026, USDA's Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service (APHIS) announced major updates to its Farm Bill animal health programs, including up to $80 million in new NADPRP funding for fiscal year 2027. The National Animal Disease Preparedness and Response Program (NADPRP) funds projects that help states, Tribes and industry prepare for and respond to high-consequence animal diseases. This explainer walks through the numbers, who can actually apply, the deadlines, and what livestock producers should do now.

The Announcement in Numbers

  • $233 million a year for APHIS' three Farm Bill animal health programs - NADPRP, the National Animal Health Laboratory Network (NAHLN) and the National Animal Vaccine and Veterinary Countermeasures Bank (NAVVCB) - up from $30 million a year, beginning in fiscal year 2026. The increase comes from the 2025 Working Families Tax Cuts Act.
  • Up to $80 million in NADPRP funding through two competitive FY27 funding opportunities: $40 million for state preparedness and $40 million for industry preparedness.
  • Open November 5, 2026; close January 7, 2027. Selected projects will begin in spring 2027.
  • The combined FY26-FY27 NADPRP spending plan also includes $15 million to continue successful ongoing projects, $15 million for ad hoc preparedness needs, and $20 million for APHIS-led initiatives to address critical gaps in animal disease response.
  • NAHLN: $10 million in noncompetitive FY26 funding for laboratories, plus a further $10 million in FY27 for operational support - the front line of early detection through rapid diagnostic testing.
  • NAVVCB: more than $85 million invested since FY2020, with over $153 million in new FY27 investments for foot-and-mouth disease (FMD) vaccine antigen concentrate and diagnostic test kits or other countermeasures.

The Two $40 Million Opportunities

OpportunityAmountWho can applyWhat it funds
NADPRP FY27 State Preparedness$40 millionState Departments of Agriculture, state animal health officials, and regional animal disease response organizationsProjects that strengthen state and regional capacity to prevent, prepare for, respond to, and recover from foreign animal disease outbreaks
NADPRP FY27 Industry Preparedness$40 millionLivestock producer organizations and veterinary organizationsProjects that enhance producer and industry readiness through improved prevention, preparedness, response, and recovery capabilities

The split matters: one window is for government animal-health capacity, the other for the producer and veterinary organizations that work directly with farms. Full details, applicant instructions and funding priorities are on the APHIS website at aphis.usda.gov/funding/nadprp.

Which Diseases This Targets

NADPRP covers high-consequence animal diseases, including New World screwworm, highly pathogenic avian influenza (HPAI), African swine fever (ASF), and foot-and-mouth disease (FMD), as well as emerging threats such as the Asian longhorned tick and Theileria orientalis Ikeda, a tick-transmitted parasite that causes a severe and currently untreatable disease in cattle. The point of the funding is readiness before an outbreak - biosecurity, training, traceability, response planning and recovery - not compensation after one.

What Livestock Producers Can Do Now

  • Talk to your producer organization before January 7. Individual farms cannot apply directly; livestock producer organizations and veterinary organizations are the eligible applicants for the industry window. If your association is considering a proposal, it needs time to put one together.
  • Check your own readiness while the window is open. Review biosecurity at livestock commingling points, visitor and vehicle protocols, and your disease response plan with your veterinarian. Keep routine herd work sharp with our Cattle Feed Ration Calculator, Livestock Weight Estimator and Veterinary Dosage Calculator.
  • State animal health officials have their own window. The $40 million state opportunity is the channel for state departments of agriculture and regional response organizations - worth flagging to your state veterinarian if your state has a gap in outbreak planning.
  • Do not wait for spring. Applications close January 7, 2027, and selected projects begin in spring 2027. The funding decisions made this winter shape which preparedness projects are running when the next disease threat arrives.

Conclusion

The headline is the scale of the change: annual funding for the three Farm Bill animal health programs jumps from $30 million to $233 million, and $80 million of NADPRP money is on the table now, split evenly between state and industry preparedness. For livestock producers the practical takeaway is narrow and time-bound - the money flows through producer organizations, veterinary organizations and state agencies, and the application window closes January 7, 2027. If your organization can use it, the conversation needs to start this month.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can individual farmers apply for NADPRP funding directly?

No. The state opportunity is for State Departments of Agriculture, state animal health officials and regional animal disease response organizations; the industry opportunity is for livestock producer organizations and veterinary organizations. Individual producers benefit through their organizations and state programs, not through a farm-level application.

How much funding is available, and when does it close?

Up to $80 million across the two FY27 NADPRP opportunities - $40 million for state preparedness and $40 million for industry preparedness. Both opened November 5, 2026 and close January 7, 2027; selected projects begin in spring 2027.

What is the $233 million figure in the announcement?

That is the new annual funding level for all three Farm Bill animal health programs together - NADPRP, NAHLN and NAVVCB - up from $30 million a year beginning in FY2026 under the 2025 Working Families Tax Cuts Act. The $80 million is the NADPRP competitive funding on offer for FY27 within that larger total.

Where can I read the official announcement?

USDA APHIS published the announcement on November 5, 2026; the full text is linked in the References section below, and funding details are at aphis.usda.gov/funding/nadprp.
