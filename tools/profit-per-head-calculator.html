Profit per Head Calculator

Calculate net profit per animal, cost of gain per kg, total operation profit, and profit margin percentage for your livestock enterprise.

This calculator breaks down all costs of production — purchase, feed, veterinary, overhead — against sale price to determine net profit per head. It also computes cost of gain ($/kg) and overall profit margin. Essential for evaluating livestock enterprise profitability.

A 50-head cattle finishing operation buys feeders at $500, spends $250 on feed, $35 vet, $40 overhead, sells at $950. Net = $950 − $825 = $125/head. Total profit = 50 × $125 = $6,250. Cost of gain = $250 ÷ 200 kg = $1.25/kg. At 13.2% margin, this is decent — but feed cost reduction or better sale price could add $3,000+.

How to Use This Calculator

Select production type. Enter purchase cost, total feed cost, vet/health cost, overhead/management, sale price, number of animals, and weight gain. Click Calculate.

About This Tool

The Profit per Head Calculator helps producers evaluate enterprise economics. Understanding cost of gain and margin percentage guides decisions on: buy vs raise, when to sell, how much to invest in quality, and whether to expand. NRC nutrient requirements drive feed cost optimization.

Applicability: This calculator is designed for global livestock operations, with particular relevance for North American and European cattle, pig, and poultry farms.

How the Calculation Works

Total cost/head = purchase + feed + vet + overhead. Net profit/head = sale price − total cost. Cost of gain = feed cost ÷ weight gain (kg). Total profit = net/head × head count. Margin = net ÷ sale × 100. Healthy margins: 10-20% for finishing cattle, 5-15% for dairy.

Real-World Example

Purchase $500 + feed $250 + vet $35 + overhead $40 = $825 cost. Sale $950. Net = $125/head. Total (50 head) = $6,250. Cost of gain = $250 ÷ 200 kg = $1.25/kg. Margin = 125/950 = 13.2%. Break-even sale price = $825/head.

Common Mistakes & Tips

Common mistakes: forgetting to include overhead costs, not accounting for death loss, using current sale price but historic purchase cost. Tip: calculate per head AND per kg of gain — the latter reveals feed efficiency. Compare your cost of gain against local benchmarks. NRC-based rations can reduce feed cost while maintaining gain.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good profit margin for livestock?

Beef finishing: 10-20% margin ($100-200/head). Dairy: $15-30/cow/month net profit. Sheep: $20-50/head. Pigs: $15-40/head. Margins vary with commodity cycles. Feed typically represents 60-75% of total cost. NRC-aligned rations optimize feed cost vs gain. Track monthly margins, not just annual.

How do I calculate cost of gain?

Cost of gain = total feed cost ÷ total weight gain. For feeders: if you spent $250/head on feed and gained 200 kg, cost = $1.25/kg. Target cost of gain: $1.00-1.50/kg for feedlot cattle. Higher than $1.80/kg indicates inefficient feeding or poor forage. NRC nutrient requirements help formulate least-cost rations that minimize cost of gain.

What is the biggest cost in livestock production?

Feed represents 60-75% of total variable cost. Then: overhead (labor, facilities, insurance) 10-15%, veterinary/health 5-10%, purchase cost (for stocker/finishing) varies. For cow-calf operations, feed is 50-60% of total cost. Reducing feed cost through better pasture, ration formulation, and waste reduction has the largest profit impact.

When is the best time to sell livestock?

Consider: (1) Price cycles — sell when market is high, (2) Cost of gain vs sale price per kg — if cost of gain exceeds value of added weight, sell now, (3) Market outlook — futures and local demand, (4) Feed availability — if feed costs will rise, sell before. Use this calculator with different sale prices to find your break-even and profit scenarios.

How can I improve profit per head?

Levers: (1) Reduce feed cost — test rations against NRC, improve pasture, reduce waste, (2) Improve gain — health program, genetics, proper nutrition, (3) Increase sale price — quality grading, direct marketing, value-added, (4) Reduce overhead — efficient facilities, reduce labor, (5) Lower death loss — vaccination, biosecurity. Even $10/head improvement on 100 head = $1,000 profit.
