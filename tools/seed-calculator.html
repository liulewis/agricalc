Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate the correct Seed rate?

To calculate the correct Seed rate: (1) Determine desired plant population (plants/ha or plants/acre) for your crop and conditions, (2) Account for germination percentage (from seed tag or test), (3) Account for expected field emergence (typically 80-95% of germination), (4) Calculate seed weight needed using thousand-kernel weight (TKW) or seeds per kg, (5) Formula: Seeding rate (kg/ha) = (Desired population × TKW) ÷ (Germination % × Field emergence % × 100). For example: wheat target 300 plants/m², TKW 40 g, germination 95%, emergence 90% → rate = (300 × 40) ÷ (0.95 × 0.90 × 100) = 140 kg/ha.

What is the ideal plant population for Seed?

Ideal plant populations for Seed depend on crop, soil fertility, moisture availability, and planting date. Common ranges: corn 60,000-90,000 plants/ha (higher for irrigated, lower for dryland), wheat 250-400 plants/m², soybeans 300,500-500,000 plants/ha, rice 100-200 plants/m² (transplanted) or 300-500 (direct-seeded), cotton 80,000-120,000 plants/ha, sunflower 40,000-70,000 plants/ha. Too high a population causes intraspecific competition, lodging, and reduced individual plant yield; too low wastes resources and reduces total yield. Local extension recommendations provide crop- and region-specific guidance.

How does row spacing affect Seed?

Row spacing affects Seed through: light interception (narrower rows achieve canopy closure faster, intercepting more sunlight and reducing weed competition), water use efficiency, airflow and disease pressure (wider rows improve airflow, reducing fungal diseases), machinery compatibility (row spacing must match planter, cultivator, and harvester equipment), and planting density. Common spacings: corn 70-76 cm (conventional) or 38-51 cm (narrow), wheat 15-20 cm (drill) or 30 cm (row), soybeans 19-38 cm (narrow) or 76 cm (row). Narrow rows generally increase yield by 5-15% in favorable conditions but may increase disease risk in humid climates.

What seed quality factors matter for Seed?

Key seed quality factors for Seed: germination percentage (minimum standards: 85% for most field crops, 90% for vegetables, 75% for some grasses), purity (percentage of pure seed vs inert matter and other crop/weed seeds — minimum 95-98%), vigor (ability to germinate under stress conditions — tested via cold test, accelerated aging), seed health (freedom from seed-borne diseases), moisture content (12-13% for safe storage of most grains), and genetic purity (for hybrid and variety seed). Always purchase certified seed with a current seed tag showing test results. High-quality seed ensures uniform emergence and optimal plant stands.

How do I calibrate my seeder for Seed?

To calibrate your seeder for Seed: (1) Determine target seeding rate (kg/ha or seeds/m), (2) Consult seeder manual for initial setting based on seed size and rate, (3) Fill seed hopper with the actual seed to be planted, (4) Drive a measured distance (e.g., 50 m) at normal planting speed with seeder operating, (5) Collect and weigh seed from a known number of rows or units, (6) Calculate actual seeding rate: Rate (kg/ha) = (Seed weight collected × 10,000) ÷ (Distance × row spacing × number of rows), (7) Adjust seeder setting up or down and repeat until rate matches target within ±5%. Recalibrate when changing seed lots, varieties, or rates. Proper calibration ensures accurate plant populations and avoids costly over- or under-seeding.
