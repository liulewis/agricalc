Grain Shrink Calculator

Quantify bushel and dollar loss when drying grain to market moisture.

Enter initial moisture, target final moisture, initial bushels, and sale price. The calculator applies the standard 1.2 shrink factor (≈1.2% bushel loss per percentage point of moisture removed) to show final bushels, bushels lost, and revenue impact.

A grower delivered 1,000 bu of corn at 25% moisture instead of drying to 15.5%. The elevator's shrink cost him 114 bushels — about $515 at $4.50/bu. Knowing shrink in advance helps decide whether to dry on-farm or sell wet.

How the Calculation Works

Standard empirical rule: Shrink % = (Initial moisture % − Final moisture %) × 1.2. Final bushels = initial bushels × (1 − shrink % ÷ 100). Bushels lost = initial − final. Revenue loss = bushels lost × price per bushel. The 1.2 factor approximates water removal plus normal handling loss.

Real-World Example

Corn from 25% to 15%: shrink = (25−15) × 1.2 = 12%. From 1,000 bu: final = 880 bu, loss = 120 bu. At $4.50/bu: $540 revenue loss.

Common Mistakes & Tips

Common mistakes: using simple percentage subtraction (this understates loss); forgetting that test weight often rises after drying; applying the wrong factor for soybeans or wheat; ignoring shrink on top of dockage. Compare drying cost vs wet discount to decide.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 1.2 shrink rule?

For every percentage point of moisture removed, bushel weight drops by about 1.2% — the extra 0.2% covers handling, broken kernels and handling losses. It is a widely used rule of thumb for marketing decisions.

Why not just subtract moisture?

Subtracting 10 points from 25%→15% suggests 10% loss. The correct formula is 11.76% (dry-matter basis), and the 1.2 rule gives 12%. The difference is the denominator: final weight is smaller, so the same water loss is a larger percentage.

Should I dry or sell wet?

Compare the on-farm drying cost (fuel + electricity + labor + equipment) with the elevator's moisture discount. If drying costs less than the discount, drying is profitable. Also consider storage time and quality.

Does the shrink factor vary by crop?

Roughly: corn uses 1.2; soybeans often use 1.3–1.4 because of their seed structure; wheat near 1.2. When in doubt, ask your elevator which formula they apply — they will tell you exactly.

What are standard target moistures?

Corn: 15% short-term, 14% long-term. Soybeans: 13%. Wheat: 13.5%. Sorghum: 14%. Storing above these raises spoilage, mold and insect risk.
