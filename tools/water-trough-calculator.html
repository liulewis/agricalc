Water Trough Calculator

Size your livestock water troughs and drinking stations based on animal type, head count, temperature, and water source. Avoid water shortages during peak drinking times.

Select animal type (cattle, sheep, pigs, horses, poultry), enter head count, temperature level, and water source (gravity or pressure). Get daily water demand, peak hourly consumption, recommended trough capacity, and number of drinking spots.

A feedlot operator with 200 cattle installed one 500 L trough, thinking it was plenty. At midday on a 35°C day, the herd rushed to drink at once. The trough emptied in 20 minutes. Crows knocked over drinkers and some cattle went without water for hours — decreasing weight gain by 12%. The calculator shows: 200 cattle × 75 L (hot factor) = 15,000 L/day. Peak hour = 2,250 L. Trough should hold 4,500 L across multiple spots, with at least 14 drinking positions.

How to Use This Calculator

Select your animal type (each has a different base daily intake), enter the number of animals, select temperature (cool <15°C, warm 15-25°C, hot >25°C), and water source (gravity needs larger troughs since refill is slower). Click Calculate for trough sizing.

About This Tool

The Water Trough Calculator prevents livestock water stress by sizing troughs correctly. Animals drink most heavily in the 2 hours after sunrise and around sunset — troughs must hold enough water for peak demand plus a 2-hour reserve. Pressure systems can refill faster; gravity systems need bigger storage.

Applicability: This calculator is designed for global livestock operations, with particular relevance for North American and European cattle, pig, and poultry farms.

How the Calculation Works

Daily requirement = Base rate × Temperature factor × Head. Base rates: cattle 50 L, sheep 5 L, pigs 15 L, horses 40 L, poultry 0.3 L per bird. Temperature: cool 0.7, warm 1.0, hot 1.5. Peak hourly = 15% of daily. Trough size = Peak hourly × 2 hours reserve. For gravity sources, add 50% to trough size.

Real-World Example

50 cattle, warm weather, pressure supply: 50 × 1.0 × 50 = 2,500 L/day. Peak hour = 375 L. Trough size = 750 L (minimum). Drinker spots = ceil(50/15) = 4 spots. On a hot day: 50 × 1.5 × 50 = 3,750 L/day, trough = 1,125 L. Always size for hot days.

Common Mistakes & Tips

Common mistakes: sizing troughs for average consumption not peak, providing only one drinking spot for large herds (cattle crowd and dominant animals push others away), not cleaning troughs regularly (algae reduces water intake), and underestimating hot-weather demand. Provide shade near troughs to reduce heat stress.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much water does a dairy cow drink per day?

A lactating dairy cow drinks 70-150 L/day depending on milk yield, temperature, and moisture content of feed. A 40 kg milk cow drinks ~100 L/day in moderate weather, up to 150 L/day in hot weather. Dry cows drink 30-50 L/day. Provide at least 10 cm of drinking space per cow and ensure water is available within 150 m of all locations.

How often should I clean livestock water troughs?

Clean troughs at least every 2 weeks in warm weather and monthly in cool weather. Algae, biofilm, and manure contamination reduce water intake by 10-30%. Scrub with a brush, use diluted bleach (1 part bleach to 32 parts water) for disinfection, and rinse thoroughly. Troughs in direct sunlight grow algae fastest — shade them.

How many waterers do I need for my herd?

General rule: cattle need 1 drinking space per 15-20 head; sheep 1 per 50; pigs 1 per 20; horses 1 per 5; poultry 1 bell drinker per 100 birds. Space waterers evenly so no animal walks more than 150 m (cattle) or 300 m (sheep/goats) to drink. In large paddocks, place troughs near shade and loafing areas.

Does temperature really affect livestock water intake that much?

Yes — dramatically. When temperatures exceed 25°C, water intake increases by 30-50%. At 35°C+, it doubles. This is because animals use water for evaporative cooling (panting). Dehydration reduces feed intake, weight gain, and milk production. Always plan trough capacity for the hottest days of the year, not the annual average.

What flow rate do I need for automatic waterers?

Automatic waterers should deliver at least 2 L/min per drinking position for cattle. This allows a cow to drink 20-30 L in 10-15 minutes. For sheep/goats, 1 L/min per position. If your supply line is too small, pressure drops during peak use and the trough can't refill. Size pipes to maintain at least 100 kPa (15 psi) at the farthest waterer.
