Hay Requirement & Feed Planning Calculator

Estimate how much hay you need to feed your livestock through a winter or dry period. Based on animal body weight and daily dry matter intake (typically 2.0-3.0% of body weight), this calculator projects total hay tons, bale count, and cost.

Select your animal type, enter the number of animals, average body weight, daily intake percentage, feeding period, and hay price. The calculator computes daily intake per animal, total hay needed in tons and bales, and the total feed cost.

Formula & How It Works

Daily Intake per Animal (lb) = Body Weight (lb) × Intake % ÷ 100

Beef cattle typically consume 2.0-2.5% of body weight in dry matter daily; dairy cows 3.0-4.0%; horses 1.5-2.5%; sheep and goats 2.5-3.5%.

Total Daily Hay (lb) = Daily Intake × Number of Animals

Total Hay (tons) = Total Daily (lb) × Feeding Days ÷ 2,000

Bale Count = Total Hay (lb) ÷ Bale Weight (50 lb assumed for small square bales)

Calculation Example

50 beef cattle averaging 1,200 lb, at 2.5% daily intake: Daily per animal = 1,200 × 0.025 = 30 lb/day. Total daily = 30 × 50 = 1,500 lb/day.

For a 180-day feeding period: Total hay = 1,500 × 180 ÷ 2,000 = 135 tons. In 50-lb bales: 270,000 ÷ 50 = 5,400 bales. At $180/ton: Total cost = 135 × 180 = $24,300 ($0.27/animal/day).

How to Use This Calculator

About This Tool

Hay is the primary forage source for most livestock operations during winter or drought periods. Accurate hay requirement planning ensures you have enough feed on hand, helps budget feed costs, and prevents costly last-minute purchases. The 2.5% body weight rule is a standard extension recommendation for dry matter intake, but actual intake varies with hay quality (lower-quality hay is consumed in smaller amounts because of slower passage), animal condition, temperature, and production stage (lactating animals need more).

Applicability: This calculator is designed for global livestock operations, with particular relevance for North American and European cattle, pig, and poultry farms.

Common Mistakes & Tips

Common mistakes: Using as-fed weight instead of dry matter — hay is typically 85-90% dry matter, so 30 lb of hay as-fed contains ~26 lb of dry matter. Underestimating feeding days — start feeding earlier than you think, especially if fall pasture runs short. Not accounting for waste — hay waste from feeding can be 5-20% depending on the feeder type; build in a buffer. Ignoring hay quality — poor-quality hay may not meet nutritional needs even if the weight is sufficient.

Frequently Asked Questions

What percentage of body weight should I use for daily intake? ▼
Mature beef cattle: 2.0-2.5% of body weight in dry matter. Dairy cows: 3.0-4.0%. Horses: 1.5-2.5%. Sheep and goats: 2.5-3.5%. Lactating, growing, or cold-stressed animals need the higher end of the range. Always use dry matter basis.
How much hay waste should I account for? ▼
Waste varies by feeding method: hay rings/wagons = 5-10% waste; feeding on ground = 15-25% waste; unrolling = 10-15%. Plan to purchase 10-15% more hay than your calculated requirement to account for waste and variability in animal intake.
Should I adjust for hay moisture content? ▼
Yes. The intake percentage is on a dry matter basis. If your hay tests 15% moisture, then 30 lb as-fed = 25.5 lb dry matter. To get 30 lb of dry matter, you need to feed 35.3 lb as-fed. Have your hay tested for moisture and quality to refine your estimates.
What units does the Hay Requirement Calculator use? ▼
Animal count unitless, body weight in lb, intake as % of body weight, feeding days unitless, hay price in $/ton. Results in lb/day, tons, bales, and $.
How do I use the Hay Requirement Calculator correctly? ▼
Start by gathering accurate input values from your farm records or measurements. Enter each value in the specified units, then click Calculate. Review all results carefully — cross-check the primary output against your own experience or extension recommendations. For best results, use actual measured values rather than estimates, and recalibrate whenever conditions change.
