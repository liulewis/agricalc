Grain Storage Costs: Storage, Drying, and Quality Loss

8 Jul 2026 Agricalc Editorial Team 7 min read

Introduction

Grain storage costs and drying decisions directly affect your bottom line: storing grain at 18% moisture without drying leads to spoilage, while over-drying to 12% costs extra fuel and reduces sale weight through moisture shrink. Properly dried and stored grain can capture 10–30% higher prices by selling outside harvest, but poor management results in discounts, dockage, or total loss.

This guide covers moisture targets by grain type, drying methods and costs, moisture shrink calculation, on-farm vs commercial storage economics, bin capacity planning, aeration design, and pest management. We also explain how to calculate the true cost of storage to decide when to sell.

Grain Storage Calculator: Moisture, Drying, and Quality

Grain must be dried to safe storage moisture: 15% for corn, 13% for soybeans, 14% for wheat, 12% for sorghum. Each point of moisture removed requires energy. The standard rule of thumb for propane-heated high-temperature dryers is $0.02 to $0.035 per bushel per percentage point of moisture removed, including fuel, electricity, and dryer depreciation. At $1.50/gal propane, the fuel alone is about $0.018–0.022/bu/point; electricity and maintenance add another $0.005–0.01.

Natural gas dryers are cheaper, roughly $0.012–0.018/bu/point. Low-temperature or natural-air drying costs less in fuel ($0.005–0.01/bu/point) but requires favorable weather and more time, and risks mold if humidity spikes.

The formula for total drying cost:

Drying cost = Bushels × (Initial moisture% − Target moisture%) × Cost per bu per point

Corn harvested at 22% moisture and dried to 15% (7 points) at $0.025/bu/point for 10,000 bu = 10,000 × 7 × 0.025 = $1,750.

Grain Storage Costs: On-Farm vs Commercial Drying

Moisture shrink is the weight lost when grain dries. The formula is:

Moisture shrink % = (Initial% − Final%) ÷ (100 − Final%) × 100

Corn from 22% to 15%: (22 − 15) ÷ (100 − 15) × 100 = 8.24% weight loss. On 10,000 bu at 56 lb/bu, that is 46,160 lb or 824 bushels lost — at $4.50/bu, $3,708 in reduced volume. This is why elevators apply moisture shrink discounts; storing wet grain and letting it dry naturally still costs you the same weight.

Storage shrink from insects, mold, and handling adds a further 0.25–0.5% per month in well-managed bins, and 1–2% per month in poorly aerated bins. Over six months, that is 1.5–3% additional loss. Quality discounts for damaged kernels (FDA > 5%), mycotoxins, or foreign material can range from $0.05 to $0.50/bu or more — and in severe cases, the grain may be rejected entirely.

Bin Design, Aeration, and Capacity Planning

A 10,000-bushel galvanized grain bin with aeration floor, fan, and unloading equipment costs approximately $35,000–$50,000 installed. Amortized over 25 years at 6% interest, the annual capital cost is roughly $2,700–$3,900, or $0.27–$0.39/bu/year if the bin is full. If you only fill it to 60% capacity, the per-bushel cost rises proportionally.

Operating costs include:

  • Aeration electricity: $0.01–0.02/bu/month (running the fan 40–80 hours/month at $0.12/kWh)
  • Pest management: $0.02–0.05/bu/year (insecticide treatment, monitoring)
  • Repairs and maintenance: $0.01–0.02/bu/year
  • Insurance: $0.005–0.01/bu/year

Total fixed+operating storage cost (excluding drying) is typically $0.04–$0.08/bu/month, or $0.24–$0.48/bu for six months.

Pest Management and Grain Preservation

Scenario: 10,000 bu corn harvested at 22% moisture, dried to 15%, stored 6 months (November–April). Harvest price: $4.20/bu. Expected April price: $4.80/bu. Propane: $1.60/gal. Bin cost: $0.32/bu/year amortized. Interest on grain value: 6% annual.

Drying: 7 points × $0.025/bu/point × 10,000 = $1,750 ($0.175/bu)
Moisture shrink: 8.24% = 824 bu lost × $4.50 (avg) = $3,708 ($0.371/bu)
Storage shrink (6 mo at 0.4%/mo): 2.4% of remaining 9,176 bu = 220 bu × $4.80 = $1,056 ($0.106/bu)
Bin amortization (6 mo): $0.16/bu × 10,000 = $1,600 ($0.16/bu)
Aeration (6 mo): $0.015/bu/mo × 6 × 10,000 = $900 ($0.09/bu)
Pest + repairs + insurance: $0.04/bu × 10,000 = $400 ($0.04/bu)
Interest (6 mo at 6% on $42,000): $1,260 ($0.126/bu)

Total storage cost: $10,674, or $1.067/bu.
Break-even April price: $4.20 + $1.067 = $5.267/bu.
At an expected April price of $4.80/bu, storage loses $0.467/bu or $4,670. The apparent $0.60/bu rally is not enough to cover the true cost of carry.

Monitoring Storage and Calculating Profit

1. Forgetting moisture shrink. This is the single largest storage cost and is often overlooked. Drying 22% corn to 15% loses 8.2% of bushels — more than all other costs combined.

2. Underestimating drying fuel. Propane prices are volatile. Lock in fuel contracts before harvest, or budget at the upper end of the range.

3. Poor aeration management. Running fans continuously in humid weather can re-wet grain and promote mold. Use automatic aeration controllers that only run when outside air is cooler and drier than grain.

4. Ignoring opportunity cost. The money tied up in stored grain could be paying down operating loans or earning interest. Include interest at your actual borrowing rate.

5. Not monitoring grain temperature. Check grain temperature every 2–4 weeks. Spoiled grain from undetected hot spots can destroy an entire bin's value.

Practical Case Study: Real-World Application

Consider a 50-hectare mixed farm in the Midwest United States implementing grain storage costs: storage, drying, and quality loss. According to the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) guidelines on sustainable agricultural practices, systematic implementation of evidence-based techniques can improve resource use efficiency by 15–25% while maintaining or increasing yields. The USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service (NRCS) recommends starting with a 10% adoption rate in the first season, scaling up as results are verified.

In practice, this means conducting a baseline assessment during the off-season, identifying the top two priority areas for improvement, and allocating a small trial area (2–5% of total acreage) to test new practices before full-scale implementation. Documenting inputs, costs, and outcomes on the trial plot provides actionable data for future decisions.

Expert Recommendations and Next Steps

Extension specialists from leading agricultural universities consistently recommend a phased approach: (1) Conduct a thorough baseline assessment, (2) prioritize high-impact, low-risk changes, (3) implement on a small trial area, (4) measure results rigorously, and (5) scale based on data. This iterative approach minimizes risk while building practical knowledge specific to your farm's unique conditions.

We recommend consulting your local agricultural extension office for region-specific recommendations, as optimal practices vary significantly by climate zone, soil type, and cropping system. Many extension services offer free soil testing, on-farm consultations, and educational workshops.

Conclusion

Effective grain storage and drying preserves quality, captures market premiums, and protects your harvest investment. Dry to the right moisture, aerate regularly, monitor temperature and insect activity, and calculate total storage cost before deciding to hold or sell.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to dry corn per bushel?

High-temperature propane drying costs $0.02–$0.035 per bushel per percentage point of moisture removed. Drying corn from 22% to 15% (7 points) costs $0.14–$0.245/bu.

What is moisture shrink in grain storage?

Moisture shrink is the weight lost when grain dries to a lower moisture content. Formula: (initial% − final%) ÷ (100 − final%) × 100. Corn from 22% to 15% shrinks 8.24%.

What is the monthly cost of storing grain?

Total storage cost (bin amortization + aeration + pest + insurance) is typically $0.04–$0.08/bu/month, excluding drying and moisture shrink.

How long can corn be stored safely?

Corn at 15% moisture and 60°F (15°C) can be stored 6–12 months. At 13% moisture and 50°F, it can store 1–2 years. Warmer or wetter grain has much shorter safe storage times.

What is the break-even price for stored grain?

Break-even = harvest price + drying cost/bu + moisture shrink value/bu + storage cost/bu + interest/bu. If the expected selling price is below this, storage loses money.
