How the Calculation Works

For a trapezoidal pile, Volume (cu ft) ≈ length × width × height ÷ 2. Weight (lb) = volume × packed density. As-fed tons = weight ÷ 2,000. Dry matter tons = as-fed tons × (100 − moisture %) ÷ 100. Typical packed densities: 35–45 lb/cu ft for corn silage at 65% moisture.

Real-World Example

100 ft × 30 ft × 12 ft pile, 40 lb/cu ft, 65% moisture: volume = 18,000 cu ft. Weight = 720,000 lb = 360 as-fed tons. Dry matter = 360 × 0.35 = 126 tons DM.

Common Mistakes & Tips

Common mistakes: using loose (not packed) density; forgetting the ÷2 trapezoid factor; ignoring that pile sloping reduces true volume; confusing as-fed with dry-matter tons. Pack carefully and sample moisture for accuracy.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a typical silage density?

Well-packed corn silage ranges 35–45 lb/cu ft (as-fed). Good packing with enough tractor weight on the pile reaches 40+; poorly packed piles can be 25–30 lb/cu ft and spoil faster.

Why divide volume by 2?

A pile has a triangular/trapezoidal cross-section, not a rectangular box. The ÷2 approximates the sloped sides. For a flat-top bunker silo with vertical walls, use full L×W×H instead.

What moisture should corn silage be?

Ideal harvest moisture is 60–65% for bunkers, 65–70% for bags. Too wet causes seepage and clostridial fermentation; too dry causes heating and molding.

What is dry matter (DM)?

Dry matter is what remains after water is removed. At 65% moisture, DM = 35%. Rations are balanced on DM, not as-fed weight, because water content varies.

How do I improve packing density?

Thin layers (6 inches), continuous packing with heavy tractors, fill in wedge shape, pack along the edges repeatedly, and seal within hours. Density directly determines spoilage and feed quality.
