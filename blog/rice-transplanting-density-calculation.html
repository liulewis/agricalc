Rice Transplanting Density: Seedling Count and Spacing Explained

7 Sep 2026 AT 10 min read

Rice feeds more than half of the world's population, and transplanting remains the dominant establishment method across Asia, parts of Africa, and Latin America. Getting transplanting density right is critical: too few hills per unit area wastes land sunlight, while too many increases seedling cost, lodging risk, and disease pressure. This guide explains how to calculate the optimal number of seedlings per hill, hill spacing, and total nursery seed requirement for your rice crop.

The Three Variables That Determine Density

Rice transplanting density is governed by three interlinked variables:

  1. Hill spacing — the distance between transplanted hills in both directions (row spacing × within-row spacing).
  2. Seedlings per hill — how many young rice plants are placed together at each hill (typically 1–4).
  3. Seedling age at transplanting — younger seedlings (12–18 days) recover faster and tiller more, while older seedlings (25–35 days) need higher density to compensate.

The product of hills per unit area and seedlings per hill gives you the total number of seedlings transplanted per hectare, which is the key metric for yield planning and nursery management.

Recommended Hill Spacing by System

Hill spacing varies widely depending on youricestablishment system and local conventions:

  • Irrigated lowland rice (Asia standard): 20 cm × 20 cm (25 hills/m²) is the most common recommendation, giving 250,000 hills/ha. Some areas use 20 cm × 15 cm (33.3 hills/m²) for higher populations.
  • System of Rice Intensification (SRI): Wider spacing of 25 cm × 25 cm (16 hills/m²) or even 30 cm × 30 cm (11.1 hills/m²), with single seedlings per hill, to maximize tillering and root development.
  • Rainfed/upland rice: 25 cm × 25 cm to 30 cm × 20 cm (13.3–16 hills/m²), reflecting lower yield potential and water availability.
  • Deepwater/floating rice: 30 cm × 30 cm or wider, as these varieties tiller extensively and grow tall.
  • Mechanized transplanting: Often 30 cm × 12–14 cm (23.8–27.8 hills/m²), matching the rowidth of transplanter machines.

How Many Seedlings per Hill?

The number of seedlings per hill depends on seedling age, variety tillering capacity, and management system:

  • Young seedlings (12–18 days, SRI method): 1 seedling per hill. Young seedlings recover quickly and produce 20–40 tillers each under good management.
  • Medium seedlings (20–25 days, conventional): 2–3 seedlings per hill. This the standard for most irrigated transplanted rice in Asia.
  • Older seedlings (30–35 days, late transplanting): 3–4 seedlings per hill to compensate for reduced tillering potential.
  • Weak tillering varieties or poor soil: Add 1 extra seedling per hill.

Calculating Total Seedlings Needed

The formula for total seedlings per hectare is:

Total seedlings/ha = (10,000 ÷ (Row spacing in m × Within-row spacing in m)) × Seedlings per hill

For conventional 20 cm × 20 cm spacing with 2 seedlings per hill: (10,000 ÷ (0.20 × 0.20)) × 2 = (10,000 ÷ 0.04) × 2 = 250,000 × 2 = 500,000 seedlings/ha.

For SRI at 25 cm × 25 cm with 1 seedling per hill: (10,000 ÷ 0.0625) × 1 = 160,000 seedlings/ha.

Nursery Seed Requirement

Once you know how many seedlings you need, calculate how much seed to sow in the nursery. Rice seed typically has 80–95% germination, and not every germinated seed produces a transplantable seedling. The general rule is:

Seed weight (kg/ha) = Total seedlings needed ÷ (Seedlings per gram × Germination % × Nursery survival %)

Rice seed weighs approximately 25–30 grams per 1,000 kernels (about 33–40 seeds per gram). For the conventional example above (500,000 seedlings/ha), assuming 35 seeds/gram, 90% germination, and 80% nursery survival:

500,000 ÷ (35 × 0.90 × 0.80) = 500,000 ÷ 25.2 = 19,841 grams = 19.8 kg seed/ha.

For SRI (160,000 seedlings/ha): 160,000 ÷ 25.2 = 6,349 grams = 6.3 kg seed/ha — a major seed saving compared to conventional transplanting or direct seeding (which often uses 80–120 kg/ha).

Worked Example: 2-Hectare Irrigated Rice Field in the Philippines

A farmer in Nueva Ecija plans to transplant a 2-hectare field using conventional spacing of 20 cm × 20 cm with 3 seedlings per hill (the local practice for 25-day-old seedlings). The seed lot has 88% germination, and the farmer expects 85% survival in the nursery. Thousand-kernel weight is 28 grams.

Step 1: Hills/ha = 10,000 ÷ (0.20 × 0.20) = 250,000 hills/ha.

Step 2: Seedlings/ha = 250,000 × 3 = 750,000 seedlings/ha.

Step 3: Seeds per gram = 1,000 ÷ 28 = 35.7 seeds/g.

Step 4: Effective seedlings per gram of seed = 35.7 × 0.88 × 0.85 = 26.7.

Step 5: Seed needed = 750,000 ÷ 26.7 = 28,090 g = 28.1 kg/ha.

Step 6: For 2 hectares: 28.1 × 2 = 56.2 kg of seed for the nursery.

Step 7: Nursery area needed: at a sowing rate of 100 g/m² (wet bed method), 56.2 kg requires 562 m² of nursery bed — roughly 5–6% of the field area.

Yield Implications of Density

Rice yield is determined by panicles per unit area, spikelets per panicle, percentage filled grains, and grain weight. Density primarily affects panicle number. Research from the International Rice Research Institute (IRRI) shows that for irrigated lowland rice, the optimal panicle density is 400–600 panicles/m². At 20 cm × 20 cm (250,000 hills/ha) with 2 seedlings/hill and good management, each hill produces 15–25 panicles, giving 3.75–6.25 million panicles/ha, or 375–625 panicles/m² — right within the optimal range.

Transplanting too few seedlings per hill or spacing hills too far apart reduces panicle number and yield. Transplanting too densely increases lodging and sheath blight risk, and reduces grain filling because of excessive shading.

Expert Recommendations and Next Steps

Extension specialists from leading agricultural universities consistently recommend a phased approach: (1) Conduct a thorough baseline assessment, (2) prioritize high-impact, low-risk changes, (3) implement on a small trial area, (4) measure results rigorously, and (5) scale based on data. This iterative approach minimizes risk while building practical knowledge specific to your farm's unique conditions.

We recommend consulting your local agricultural extension office for region-specific recommendations, as optimal practices vary significantly by climate zone, soil type, and cropping system. Many extension services offer free soil testing, on-farm consultations, and educational workshops.

Conclusion

Rice transplanting density is a balancing act between hill spacing, seedlings per hill, and seedling age. Conventional systems use 20 cm × 20 cm with 2–3 seedlings per hill, while SRI uses wider spacing with single young seedlings. Proper density sets the foundation for high yields and efficient use of seed, labor, and land.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the standard spacing for transplanted rice?

A: The most common spacing for irrigated lowland transplanted rice is 20 cm × 20 cm (250,000 hills per hectare), with 2–3 seedlings per hill. SRI methods use wider spacing of 25 cm × 25 cm or 30 cm × 30 cm with single seedlings. Mechanized transplanters typically use 30 cm row spacing with 12–14 cm between hills.

Q: How many rice seedlingshould I plant per hill?

A: Use 1 seedling per hill for young (12–18 day) SRI seedlings, 2–3 for conventional 20–25 day seedlings, and 3–4 for older (30+ day) seedlings or weak-tillering varieties. More seedlings per hill compensates for reduced tillering but increases seed cost and lodging risk.

Q: How much seedo I need for a rice nursery per hectare?

A: Conventional transplanting requires about 20–30 kg of seed per hectare of field, depending on spacing, seedlings per hill, and germination. SRI requires only 5–7 kg/ha due to single seedlings and wider spacing. Direct-seeded rice uses much more, typically 80–120 kg/ha.

Q: Does wider spacing reduce rice yield?

A: Not necessarily. SRI research shows that wider spacing (25 cm × 25 cm or 30 cm × 30 cm) with single young seedlings can produce equal or higher yields than conventional dense planting, because each plant produces more tillers and larger panicles. The key is using young seedlings and good water, nutrient, and weed management.

Q: How do I calculate hills per hectare from spacing?

A: Divide 10,000 (square meters in a hectare) by the product of row spacing and within-row spacing in meters. For 20 cm × 20 cm: 10,000 ÷ (0.20 × 0.20) = 250,000 hills/ha. For 25 cm × 25 cm: 10,000 ÷ (0.25 × 0.25) = 160,000 hills/ha.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the optimal rice transplanting density per hectare?

Optimal rice transplanting density is 20-33 hills/m² (200,000-330,000 hills/ha), with 2-3 seedlings per hill. Common spacing is 20×20 cm (25 hills/m²) or 20×15 cm (33 hills/m²). A plant population calculator from row spacing and seed spacing helps compute exact hills per hectare for your geometry.

How does transplanting density affect rice yield?

Below 15 hills/m², yield drops due to insufficient tillers. Above 40 hills/m², lodging and disease increase, and yield plateaus or declines. The sweet spot for irrigated lowland rice is 25-33 hills/m². Use a seeding rate calculator by germination rate to match nursery area to field density.

How many seedlings do I need per hectare of rice?

At 25 hills/m² × 2.5 seedlings/hill = 62.5 seedlings/m² = 625,000 seedlings/ha. Account for 10-15% mortality during transplanting, so sow for 700,000-720,000 seedlings/ha. Nursery area is typically 1/10 to 1/8 of field area for wet-bed nurseries.

What spacing is best for mechanical transplanters?

Mechanical rice transplanters typically use 30×12 cm (27.8 hills/m²) or 30×14 cm (23.8 hills/m²) with 3-5 seedlings per hill. Mat-type nurseries require precise seeding rates. Use a seed spacing calculator for vegetable transplanting principles adapted to rice tray nurseries.
