USDA's FIELDS Fertilizer Program: $573 Million in Federal Funding, Explained for Farmers

6 Nov 2026 Agricalc Editorial Team 6 min read

Related Calculators: fertilizer calculator · fertilizer converter · organic fertilizer calculator

On October 22, 2026, USDA announced the first project selections under its FIELDS Program (Fertilizer Investment & Expansion for Long-Term Domestic Supply). Eight fertilizer manufacturing projects were selected to move forward, backed by approximately $573 million in federal funding inside a total proposed investment of more than $1.34 billion. For farmers, this is not a grant program you can apply to - it is a supply-side investment. This explainer walks through the numbers, the realistic timeline, and what the new capacity could - and could not - do to the fertilizer prices you pay.

The Announcement in Numbers

  • 8 projects selected across 16 states, covering nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium and sulfur fertilizers.
  • About $573 million in proposed USDA funding (the project-by-project figures sum to roughly $573.2 million).
  • More than $1.34 billion in total proposed investment, with the remaining roughly $771 million coming from the companies and their partners.
  • About 1.08 million tons of expected annual domestic production and capacity once all projects are operational - more than 1 million tons of new annual capacity.
  • FIELDS was launched in July 2026 as a $500 million program; the selections exceed that figure because USDA redirected additional money recovered from earlier Fertilizer Production Expansion Program (FPEP) projects that never signed agreements.
  • The program is administered by USDA Rural Development's Rural Business-Cooperative Service using Commodity Credit Corporation authorities.

The spread matters for farmers: this is not a single mega-plant. It includes modular ammonia units sited in farming regions, a new sulfate of potash plant in New Mexico, sulfur capacity in Illinois, and production from agricultural byproducts in the West and Midwest.

When Does the New Fertilizer Actually Arrive?

Not this season. "Selected to move forward" means the projects enter the FIELDS funding process - final agreements, financing, permitting and construction still have to happen. USDA officials said at the announcement that the projects are expected to break ground within weeks and be completed in 2027 or 2028. Only then does the headline figure - more than 1 million tons of new annual capacity, about 1.08 million tons in total - start reaching the market.

Practical translation: fertilizer you buy for fall 2026 and spring 2027 was priced by today's supply, not by these projects. Budget the 2027 season on current market prices, and treat anything FIELDS delivers as a 2027-2028 story.

What This Could Mean for Fertilizer Prices

USDA's economic analysis estimates that, once the projects are operational, the additional production could reduce nitrogen fertilizer prices by 4.4% and potash prices by 5.3%, while increasing domestic nitrogen production by 3.7% and potash production by nearly 40%. Two cautions belong next to those numbers:

  • They are modeled estimates, not promises. They describe what USDA economists project if the capacity is built and operates as planned. No one is guaranteeing a specific retail price at your local dealer.
  • Your delivered price has other drivers. Natural gas costs, global trade flows, transport and local dealer competition all move the price you actually pay, and they can swamp a single-digit national effect in any given season.

The structural point is still real: USDA notes that four companies control about 77% of North American nitrogen capacity and two companies about 90% of potash. More domestic plants, in more places, means more suppliers bidding for your business and less exposure to import disruptions. That is a reasonable expectation of direction - steadier supply over time - not a dated price forecast.

What Farmers Can Do Now

  • Do not pre-spend the savings. Keep 2027 fertilizer budgeting at current prices; if prices ease in 2027-2028, that is upside, not a plan.
  • Buy nutrients by need, not by habit. The cheapest ton is the one your soil test says you do not need. Turn soil-test recommendations into exact product amounts per field with our fertilizer calculator, and use the fertilizer converter when comparing products with different analyses or units.
  • Watch the projects near you. New capacity in your region - for example TalusAg's modular ammonia units across seven Midwestern and Southern states - can matter more for your local basis and availability than national averages suggest.

Conclusion

FIELDS puts about $573 million of federal money behind more than $1.34 billion of fertilizer manufacturing, and the prize is real: roughly 1.08 million tons of new annual domestic capacity expected across 2027-2028. But it is a medium-term supply story, not a discount on this season's fertilizer. Plan 2027 at today's prices, apply by soil test and measured rates, and let the new plants improve your position when they actually come online.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can farmers apply for FIELDS funding directly?

No. FIELDS funds fertilizer manufacturers, not individual farms, and there is no farm-level application. The benefit to farmers is indirect: more domestic production capacity and more competition among suppliers once the projects are operating.

How much new fertilizer supply does this add?

About 1.08 million tons of annual production capacity across the eight projects - more than 1 million tons a year of nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium and sulfur products - once all projects are completed and operational, which USDA officials expect in 2027-2028.

Will fertilizer get cheaper in 2027 because of FIELDS?

Not because of this announcement alone. USDA's economic analysis estimates the added production could reduce nitrogen fertilizer prices by 4.4% and potash prices by 5.3% once the projects are operational, but those are modeled estimates, not guaranteed retail prices, and the new capacity arrives in 2027-2028 at the earliest.

Where can I read the official announcement?

USDA published the project selections on October 22, 2026; the full text is linked in the References section below. The FIELDS program is administered by USDA Rural Development's Rural Business-Cooperative Service.
