Cold Storage Capacity Calculator

Size a cooler or cold room for apples, potatoes, onions and other produce.

Enter the cold room length, width and height, the product loading density, the stacking (use) factor, and the weight of each box. The calculator estimates total volume, storage capacity in tons, number of boxes, and recommended refrigeration tonnage.

A small packhouse built a 40×20×15 ft cooler but sized the refrigeration for half the expected load. On the first fall it could not pull apples down to 34°F fast enough, and a trailer load spoiled. A capacity check before purchase would have caught the undersized unit.

How the Calculation Works

Volume = L × W × H. Capacity (lb) = volume × loading density × stacking factor. Tons = lb ÷ 2,000. Boxes = capacity lb ÷ box weight. Refrigeration estimate ≈ capacity lb ÷ 12,000 (rule of thumb: 1 ton refrigeration per ~12,000 lb product). Loading densities: apples ~40, potatoes ~45, onions ~40 lb/cu ft.

Real-World Example

40×20×15 ft room = 12,000 cu ft. At 35 lb/cu ft and 0.7 use factor: capacity = 294,000 lb = 147 tons. At 40 lb/box: 7,350 boxes. Refrigeration ≈ 24.5 tons.

Common Mistakes & Tips

Common mistakes: using empty volume instead of usable (aisles and pallet gaps); ignoring stacking factor; undersizing refrigeration for peak pull-down; using wrong product density. Plan for worst-case peak, not average.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a stacking factor?

Not every cubic foot is filled with product — aisles, pallet gaps, door clearance and air ducts take space. A 0.7 factor means 70% of volume actually holds product.

How much refrigeration do I need?

Rule of thumb: 1 ton of refrigeration (12,000 BTU/h) per ~12,000 lb of product for pull-down. Add 25% for doors, people and peak heat. Have a refrigeration engineer size the final unit.

What temperature for apples?

Most apples store 30–34°F at 90–95% relative humidity. Potatoes: 45°F (too cold causes sweetening). Onions: 32°F with good air circulation. Each crop has its own range.

Why use product-specific density?

Apples, potatoes and onions pack differently. Denser products hold more weight per cubic foot. Using one average under- or over-states capacity and refrigeration needs.

Should I use all the volume?

No. Reserve aisles for forklift access, leave space for air flow around pallets, and keep the evaporator unobstructed. Over-packing causes hot spots and uneven cooling.
