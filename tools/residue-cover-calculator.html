Crop Residue Cover Calculator

Residue cover is the single number that decides whether a field is protected or exposed. This calculator starts from your previous crop and grain yield, works out how much residue the crop left, then applies the retention rate of your tillage implements to show what is actually left on the surface at planting — and whether that is enough to control erosion.

What it answers: Residue produced (kg/ha), residue remaining after tillage, soil surface cover (%), an erosion risk rating, and whether the field still meets the 30% cover threshold used to define conservation tillage.

Worked example: Corn at 9 t/ha leaves about 9,000 kg/ha of residue and covers roughly 93% of the surface. One mouldboard pass buries almost all of it — cover drops to about 24%, well under the 30% conservation threshold.

How to Use This Calculator

Select the crop you harvested, enter the grain yield, and leave residue at 0 unless you have actually measured it. Then choose how many tillage passes you plan and which implement does most of the work. Click Calculate to see the cover that will be left at planting.

About This Tool

Soil covered by residue absorbs raindrop impact, slows runoff and keeps moisture in the seedbed. The widely used threshold is 30% cover at planting: at or above that, a field qualifies as conservation tillage; at 15% or less it counts as intensive tillage and erosion risk rises sharply.

How the Calculation Works

Residue produced (kg/ha) = grain yield (t/ha) × 1,000 × residue-to-grain ratio (maize 1.0, sorghum 1.1, wheat and small grains 1.3, rice 1.4, soybean 1.5). After tillage: residue remaining = residue produced × (retention per pass) ^ number of passes, with retention of 0.95 for no-till, 0.80 for vertical tillage, 0.70 for chisel, 0.55 for a disc and 0.10 for a mouldboard plough. Cover (%) = 100 × (1 − e^(−k × residue remaining)), where k is 0.00030 for maize, 0.00032 for sorghum, 0.00062 for wheat and small grains, 0.00060 for rice and 0.00045 for soybean.

Worked Example

Maize at 9 t/ha: residue = 9 × 1,000 × 1.0 = 9,000 kg/ha. Two disc passes: 9,000 × 0.55² = 2,723 kg/ha. Cover = 100 × (1 − e^(−0.00030 × 2,723)) = 55.8% — still conservation tillage. Swap the disc for one mouldboard pass and cover falls to about 24%, below the threshold.

Practical Tips

  • 30% cover at planting is the standard conservation-tillage line; 15% or less is classed as intensive tillage.
  • Soybean residue breaks down fast — a field at 80% after harvest can be below 30% by the following spring.
  • A chisel or vertical-tillage tool keeps far more residue on top than a disc; a mouldboard plough buries almost all of it.
  • Measure real cover with a 30 m line-transect, counting residues under marked points, or with a photo-based app.
  • On cold, poorly drained ground, cover above 50% can delay soil warming and slow early growth.

Frequently Asked Questions

What counts as residue cover?

It is the percentage of the soil surface covered by crop residue — stalks, leaves and straw — when viewed from directly above. Everything else is bare soil exposed to raindrop impact.

Why is 30% the target?

Around 30% cover is the point where erosion drops sharply, roughly halving soil loss compared with bare ground. It is the standard used to define conservation tillage and to qualify for many soil-health programmes.

How much residue does a maize crop leave?

Roughly one tonne of residue per tonne of grain: a 9 t/ha maize crop leaves about 9 t/ha of dry residue, which is enough to cover well over 80% of the surface if it is left undisturbed.

Does more residue always mean better yield?

No. Heavy residue keeps moisture in and builds organic matter, but on cold, wet soils it slows warming in spring. The aim is enough cover to control erosion without delaying planting or emergence.
