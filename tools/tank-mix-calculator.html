Calculate your sprayer application rate, acres per tank, and exact chemical quantities for each tank load. This tool combines sprayer calibration (GPA from nozzle output, spacing, and speed) with tank mix calculations for multiple chemicals.

Formula & How It Works

Application Rate (GPA) = (Nozzle Output GPM × 5940) ÷ (Nozzle Spacing inches × Speed mph)

The constant 5940 converts gallons per minute, inches, and miles per hour into gallons per acre (43,560 sq ft ÷ 12 in/ft × 60 min/hr ÷ 1,000 = 217.8; the standard formula uses 5,940).

Acres per Tank = Tank Size (gal) ÷ Application Rate (gal/acre)

Chemical per Tank = Label Rate (qt/acre) × Acres per Tank

Calculation Example

A sprayer with 0.5 GPM nozzles spaced 20 inches apart, traveling at 8 mph: Application Rate = (0.5 × 5940) ÷ (20 × 8) = 2970 ÷ 160 = 18.56 gal/acre.

With a 500-gallon tank: Acres per Tank = 500 ÷ 18.56 = 26.94 acres. If Chemical A is applied at 1 qt/acre: 1 × 26.94 = 26.94 qt per tank.

How to Use This Calculator

1. Calibrate your sprayer by catching output from one nozzle for 30 seconds, convert to GPM. 2. Measure nozzle spacing along the boom. 3. Enter your typical field speed. 4. Input tank size and the area you need to cover. 5. Add each chemical's label rate per acre. Click Calculate to see the application rate and per-tank chemical amounts.

About This Tool

The Tank Mix Calculator is essential for precision pesticide and fertilizer application. Proper calibration ensures you apply the correct labeled rate, avoiding both under-application (poor pest control) and over-application (wasted product, environmental risk, and illegal residue levels).

Applicability: Applicable to pest management programs in North American and European cropping systems.

Common Mistakes & Tips

Common errors: Forgetting to account for nozzle wear — replace nozzles when output varies more than 10% from new. Using speedometer reading instead of actual measured field speed (slip and terrain reduce real speed). Mixing chemicals in the wrong order — always fill tank half-full with water, add products in label-recommended order, then top off.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the 5940 constant mean? ▼
5940 is a unit conversion constant: (43,560 sq ft/acre × 12 in/ft) ÷ (60 min/hr × 1,000) simplifies the GPA formula when nozzle output is in GPM, spacing in inches, and speed in mph.
How do I measure nozzle output? ▼
Catch the output from one nozzle for 30 seconds using a graduated container. Multiply ounces by 0.0078125 to get gallons, then double for GPM (since you measured 30 seconds). Repeat for several nozzles and average them.
Can I mix more than two chemicals? ▼
Yes, simply add additional chemical rate fields conceptually. Always perform a jar test first to check compatibility, and follow the label order of addition (W-A-L-E-S: Wettable powders, Agitation, Liquids, Emulsifiable concentrates, Surfactants).
What units does the Tank Mix Calculator use? ▼
Nozzle output in GPM, spacing in inches, speed in mph, tank size in gallons, area in acres, chemical rates in qt/acre. Results in GPA, acres/tank, and qt/tank.
How do I use the Tank Mix Calculator correctly? ▼
Start by gathering accurate input values from your farm records or measurements. Enter each value in the specified units, then click Calculate. Review all results carefully — cross-check the primary output against your own experience or extension recommendations. For best results, use actual measured values rather than estimates, and recalibrate whenever conditions change.
