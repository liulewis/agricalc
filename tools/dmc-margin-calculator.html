About This Tool

The Dairy Margin Coverage (DMC) Calculator estimates indemnities and premiums under USDA's Dairy Margin Coverage program for 2026–2031. It applies the OBBBA rules — a 6-million-pound Tier 1 cap, buy-up coverage from $4.00 to $9.50/cwt (Tier 2 tops out at $8.00/cwt), the official FSA premium schedule, the 25% premium discount for the six-year lock-in election, and the $100 annual administrative fee. Applicability: For U.S. dairy operations with established production history that enroll through their county FSA office. Indemnities here are estimated from a single annual margin; actual DMC payments are made monthly when the USDA-announced margin falls below your elected coverage.

How the Calculation Works

Actual margin = all-milk price − feed cost ($/cwt). Payment rate = max(coverage level − actual margin, 0). Tier 1 pounds = the first 6,000,000 lbs of production history; Tier 2 pounds = any production history above 6,000,000 lbs. Covered hundredweight = (tier pounds ÷ 100) × coverage percentage. Annual indemnity = payment rate × total covered cwt. Premium = [(Tier 1 rate × Tier 1 lbs ÷ 100) + (Tier 2 rate × Tier 2 lbs ÷ 100)] × coverage percentage; the six-year lock-in multiplies the premium by 0.75; then the $100 annual administrative fee is added. Net benefit = annual indemnity − total premium.

Real-World Example

A dairy with 5,000,000 lbs of production history (all Tier 1) elects $9.50 coverage on 95% of production and locks in for 2026–2031. All-milk price $18.00/cwt, feed cost $11.00/cwt. Actual margin = 18.00 − 11.00 = $7.00/cwt. Payment rate = max(9.50 − 7.00, 0) = $2.50/cwt. Covered cwt = 5,000,000 ÷ 100 × 95% = 47,500 cwt. Annual indemnity = 2.50 × 47,500 = $118,750. Premium = 0.15 × 50,000 × 95% = $7,125; lock-in discount: $7,125 × 0.75 = $5,343.75; plus the $100 annual fee = $5,443.75. Net benefit = 118,750 − 5,443.75 = $113,306.25.

Common Mistakes & Tips

Common mistakes include entering current-year marketings instead of the 2021–2023 production history, assuming the Tier 1 cap is still 5 million pounds (it rose to 6 million for 2026), using your farm's own margin instead of the national all-milk price and standardized feed cost, forgetting the $100 annual administrative fee, choosing a coverage percentage that scales premiums as well as indemnities, and assuming Tier 2 can be covered above $8.00/cwt.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does DMC actually protect?

DMC protects the national milk margin — the all-milk price minus the national average feed cost (corn, soybean meal and alfalfa hay). When the USDA-announced monthly margin falls below your elected coverage level, you are paid the difference times your covered production. It does not cover your farm's individual costs beyond the standardized feed ration.

How are Tier 1 and Tier 2 pounds split?

Tier 1 covers the first 6,000,000 lbs of your established production history at low buy-up premiums (for example $0.15/cwt at the $9.50 level). Tier 2 covers any pounds above 6,000,000 at much higher premiums (up to $1.8130/cwt at $8.00). Tier 2 coverage tops out at $8.00/cwt — you cannot elect $8.50 or $9.50 on Tier 2 milk.

Is the six-year lock-in worth it?

Locking in during the 2026 enrollment fixes your coverage elections for 2026–2031 and cuts premiums by 25% every year. For most dairies it pays — the discount is pure savings on a premium you would pay anyway. The trade-off is that you cannot change coverage levels later if margins or your herd size shift. The $100 annual administrative fee is still charged each year and is not discounted.

How is my production history determined?

All operations enrolling for 2026 establish a new production history: the highest annual milk marketings in 2021, 2022 or 2023 for existing dairies (those marketing milk on or before January 1, 2023). Newer operations use their first year of monthly marketings, even for a partial year.

What is the $100 annual fee?

Every participating dairy pays a $100 administrative fee each coverage year, on top of premiums. It is not discounted by the lock-in and applies whether or not you receive indemnities.
