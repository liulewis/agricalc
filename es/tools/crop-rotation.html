Input total farm area, rotation system, crop area ratios, and starting year. Get crop areas, planting/harvest schedules, and system benefits. Rotation improves soil, reduces pests, and spreads risk.

You grew corn continuously on the same 50-hectare field for 5 years. Byear 3, corn rootworm resistance required two insecticide applications per year. Byear 5, soil organic matter hadropped from 3.5% to 2.2%, and yields declined from 10 t/ha to 7.5 t/ha. You switched to a corn-soybean-wheat rotation. In year 1 of rotation, corn yield jumped back to 9.5 t/ha with zero insecticide — the "rotation effect" (break in pest cycle + improved soil) added 2 t/ha. Over 5 years, the rotation system generated $120/ha/year more net profit than continuous corn, despite lower corn acreage. Rotation is not just "good for the soil" — it is more profitable.

Preguntas Frecuentes

How do I calculate the optimal Crop Rotation for my field?

To calculate optimal Crop Rotation: (1) Determine target yield based on soil potential, climate, and management level, (2) Calculate input requirements (seed, fertilizer, water) needed to support that yield, (3) Account for efficiency factors (germination 85-95%, fertilizer use efficiency, irrigation efficiency), (4) Consider field constraints (slope, soil type, drainage), (5) Compare scenarios with different input levels to find the most profitable combination. Use local extension yield goals and budget guides. The optimal rate is where marginal revenue equals marginal cost, not necessarily maximum yield.

What factors most affect Crop Rotation results?

Key factors affecting Crop Rotation: soil fertility and pH (most limiting nutrient determines yield ceiling), water availability (timing and amount during critical growth stages), weather (temperature, rainfall, frost dates), pest and disease pressure, crop variety/genetics, planting date and population, and management timeliness. Even with perfect inputs, drought at flowering can reduce corn yield by 30-50%. Diversify risk through crop rotation, resistant varieties, and irrigation where possible. Keep records to identify your farm's specific yield-limiting factors over years.

How can I improve Crop Rotation without increasing inputs?

Improve Crop Rotation through: better timing (plant at optimal soil temperature, apply nutrients when crop needs them), improved soil health (cover crops, reduced tillage, organic matter increase water and nutrient retention), precision agriculture (variable rate application targets inputs where needed), pest scouting (early detection prevents yield loss), crop rotation (breaks pest cycles, improves soil), and selecting adapted varieties (local trial data identifies best performers). Many farmers increase yield 10-20% through better management alone without additional inputs.

What is the difference between potential and actual Crop Rotation?

Potential Crop Rotation is the maximum achievable with unlimited water, nutrients, and no pests — determined by genetics and solar radiation. Actual Crop Rotation is what you harvest, reduced by water stress, nutrient limitations, pests, weather, and management gaps. The gap between potential and actual is typically 30-60% for rainfed crops, 15-30% for irrigated. Yield gap analysis identifies which factor is most limiting. Closing the gap through better management is usually more profitable than simply adding more inputs. Water is the most common limiting factor globally.

How do I use Crop Rotation results for farm planning?

Use Crop Rotation results for: budget preparation (estimate revenue = yield × price), input purchasing (buy seed/fertilizer based on planned acreage and rates), marketing decisions (store or sell based on expected production), crop insurance (choose coverage level based on APH yield), equipment sizing (match harvester capacity to expected yield), and labor planning (schedule harvest labor). Keep 3-5 years of yield records by field to establish realistic yield goals. Yield maps from combines show spatial variability, enabling site-specific management for future seasons.
