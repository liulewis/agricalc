Calcular FCR (feed conversion ratio), feed efficiency, and profitability analysis for livestock and aquaculture.

Calcular Índice de Conversión Alimenticia (FCR), feed efficiency, and profitability analysis for livestock and aquaculture. Input feed consumed, weight gain or production, feed price, and product price. Get FCR, feed cost, product value, gross profit, profit per kg, and feed cost ratio. FCR is the most important efficiency metric in animal production.

Your pig farm had an FCR of 3.2:1, and you thought "that is just how pigs grow." A consultant pointed outhat your feed was stored in a leaky bin — 10% was spoiled by mold, and your feeder wasting another 8% through spillage. Real feed consumed by pigs was 22% less than what you bought. Real FCR was 2.5:1 — but your economic FCR (feed purchased / weight gain) was 3.2:1. Fixing the bin and feedereduced economic FCR to 2.7:1, saving $8 per pig. With 500 pigs per batch and 4 batches/year, that is $16,000/year in savings. FCR is not just genetics and feed quality — it is also feed management and waste reduction.

Preguntas Frecuentes

What is the optimal Fcr for different livestock species?

Optimal Fcr varies by species and stage: cattle growing calves gain 0.8-1.2 kg/day, mature cows maintain at 2% body weight DM/day; pigs grow 0.7-0.9 kg/day with FCR 2.5-3.0; poultry broilers reach 2 kg in 5-6 weeks with FCR 1.6-1.9; sheep lambs gain 0.2-0.3 kg/day. Genetics, nutrition, health, and environment all influence performance. Regular weighing and record-keeping identify underperforming animals. Consult NRC (National Research Council) publications for species-specific nutrient requirements at each production stage.

How do nutrition and Fcr relate?

Nutrition is the primary driver of Fcr. Energy fuels maintenance and growth; protein builds muscle and supports reproduction; minerals/vitamins are essential for bone, immune function, and metabolism. Deficiencies limit growth even with adequate energy. Cattle on low-protein forage (<7% CP) cannot digest fiber efficiently, reducing intake and gain. Ration balancing using forage analysis and NRC standards optimizes Fcr while minimizing feed costs. For ruminants, forage quality determines intake potential — high-quality forage supports higher intake and gain.

What health issues affect Fcr?

Health issues reducing Fcr: internal parasites (reduce nutrient absorption 10-30%), respiratory disease (shipping fever costs $50-100+/animal), digestive disorders (acidosis on high-grain diets), lameness (reduces feed intake), reproductive failures, metabolic diseases (milk fever, ketosis). Prevention through vaccination, parasite control, proper nutrition, biosecurity, and stress reduction is far more cost-effective than treatment. Regular health monitoring and prompt veterinary intervention minimize production losses. A good health program returns $3-5 for every $1 invested.

How do I measure and track Fcr?

Measure Fcr by: regular weighing (cattle every 30-60 days, pigs every 2-4 weeks), using calibrated scales or weight tapes (cattle heart girth estimates weight within 5-10%), recording group/individual weights, calculating ADG = (final − initial) ÷ days, tracking feed intake for FCR = feed ÷ gain, maintaining records in spreadsheets or herd software. Compare actual performance against breed standards and historical data to identify problems early. For pasture cattle, use periodic weight checks to evaluate forage quality and stocking rate.

What environmental factors influence Fcr?

Environmental factors: temperature (heat stress above 25-30°C reduces feed intake 10-30%; cold stress increases energy needs), humidity (exacerbates heat stress), ventilation (poor air quality causes respiratory disease), stocking density (overcrowding increases stress, aggression, disease), lighting (proper cycles improve poultry growth/reproduction), water quality (clean water essential — intake correlates with feed intake). THI >72 indicates mild heat stress in cattle, >78 severe. Provide shade, fans, sprinklers, windbreaks, and clean water to maximize genetic potential for growth.
