Frequently Asked Questions

How much water does drip irrigation save compared to sprinklers?

Drip irrigation typically saves 30-60% of water compared to sprinkler systems, and up to 70% compared to flood irrigation. The savings come from reduced evaporation, no wind drift, targeted root-zone application, and higher application efficiency (85-95% vs 60-80% for sprinklers). On a 10-hectare farm, this can mean saving 5-15 million liters per season.

How do I calculate the cost savings from drip irrigation?

Calculate annual savings by comparing: Water Cost Savings = (Water Used Old Method - Water Used Drip) × Water Price; Energy Savings = (Pumping Hours Old - Pumping Hours Drip) × Energy Rate; Fertilizer Savings = (Fertilizer Used Old - Fertilizer Used Drip) × Fertilizer Price; Labor Savings = (Labor Hours Old - Labor Hours Drip) × Labor Rate. Use an irrigation cost per hectare calculator to model all variables.

What is the typical payback period for a drip irrigation system?

Payback periods range from 1-5 years depending on crop value, water cost, energy cost, and system size. High-value vegetable crops with expensive water/energy often pay back in 1-2 years. Field crops may take 3-5 years. Calculate: Payback Period = System Installation Cost ÷ Annual Net Savings. Include any government subsidies or grants in your calculation.

Does drip irrigation save fertilizer too?

Yes. Drip irrigation with fertigation can reduce fertilizer use by 20-50% by delivering nutrients directly to the root zone in precise amounts, reducing leaching and runoff. Nutrients are applied in small, frequent doses that match crop uptake, rather than in large broadcast applications that are prone to loss. This also reduces groundwater contamination risk.
