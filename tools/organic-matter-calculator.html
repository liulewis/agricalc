How to Use This Calculator

Enter the soil test OM percentage, the depth you sampled (typically 15–20 cm), bulk density from a core test, soil texture and field area. Click Calculate to see OM stock, SOC and the N your soil releases each year.

About This Tool

This calculator uses the standard extension relationship: OM stock (t/ha) = OM% × bulk density (g/cm³) × depth (cm) × 0.1. SOC is roughly 58% of OM (Van Bemmelen factor). Annual N mineralization ≈ OM% × 20 lb N/acre, adjusted by texture (sands mineralize faster, clays slower).

Applicability: Designed for temperate and subtropical soils, applicable to farming systems across North America and Europe.

How the Calculation Works

OM (t/ha) = OM% × BD × depth × 0.1. SOC (t C/ha) = OM × 0.58. Annual N mineralization ≈ OM% × texture rate (20–30 kg N/ha per 1% OM), converted from lb/acre extension values (× 0.4536).

Real-World Example

A loam with 3.5% OM, BD 1.3 g/cm³, sampled to 20 cm. OM stock = 3.5 × 1.3 × 20 × 0.1 = 9.1 t/ha. SOC = 9.1 × 0.58 = 5.3 t C/ha. Annual N mineralization ≈ 3.5 × 25 × 0.4536 ≈ 40 kg N/ha/yr.

Common Mistakes & Tips

Common mistakes: using 0–15 cm OM% for deeper calculations, forgetting to adjust bulk density for compaction, applying the same mineralization rate in all climates, and treating total OM as plant-available in year one. Only 1–4% of total OM mineralizes per year.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much organic matter is good for soil?

Most mineral soils target 3–5% OM in the top 15 cm. Sandy soils typically hold 1–3%, loams 3–6%, and high-clay or peaty soils can exceed 8%. Less than 2% topsoil OM usually means degraded soil needing cover crops and manure.

What is the difference between OM and SOC?

Soil organic matter (SOM) includes all organic material; soil organic carbon (SOC) is the carbon fraction, typically about 58% of OM (Van Bemmelen factor). To convert: SOC = OM × 0.58, or OM = SOC ÷ 0.58 ≈ SOC × 1.724.

How much N is released from organic matter each year?

Roughly 1–4% of total organic N mineralizes annually. As a rule of thumb, each 1% of OM in the top 15 cm releases about 10–25 kg N/ha/yr, depending on texture, temperature and moisture. This calculator uses 20–30 kg N/ha per 1% OM adjusted for texture.

Does adding compost really increase organic matter?

Yes, but slowly. Adding 10 t/ha of compost (about 25% OM) adds only ~2.5 t OM/ha; decomposition loses most of it in the first year. Long-term build-up requires annual additions combined with reduced tillage and cover crops.

How deep should I sample for organic matter?

Sample the plow layer (0–15 or 0–20 cm) for routine OM and nutrient management. For carbon accounting or deeper stratification, sample in layers: 0–15, 15–30, 30–60 cm. Surface OM can be 2–3 times higher than subsoil, so depth must match your calculation.
