How to Use This Calculator

Record the fuel tank level before and after an operation, or use your fuel invoice for the season, and divide by the hectares or acres covered. Enter those totals, the fuel price, and select the operation type to get per-area fuel intensity and a rating.

How the Calculation Works

L/ha = total fuel ÷ area. gal/acre = L/ha × 0.1069. $/ha = L/ha × price. For 1,200 L over 80 ha at $1.50/L: 15 L/ha = 1.60 gal/acre = $22.5/ha, total $1,800. The efficiency rating compares your intensity to typical values for the selected operation. Common mistakes: mixing liters and gallons, forgetting that harvest consumes more than planting, and ignoring idle consumption (0.5-1 gal/hr).

Frequently Asked Questions

How much fuel does tillage use per hectare?

Conventional tillage (plowing + disking) typically uses 18-25 L/ha, while reduced or no-till can drop to 6-12 L/ha because it eliminates the plow and multiple secondary passes.

Which operation burns the most fuel?

Harvesting, especially self-propelled combines, is typically 15-30 L/ha or more, followed by heavy primary tillage. Planting and spraying are the lightest at 6-12 L/ha.

How can I cut farm fuel use?

Switch to reduced/no-till, reduce passes, maintain tire pressure and ballast, avoid unnecessary idling, match tractor size to the implement, and use GPS guidance to skip overlap. Each 10% fuel saving compounds over the farm's whole acreage.

How do I convert L/ha to gal/acre?

Multiply L/ha by 0.1069 to get US gallons per acre. Conversely, multiply gal/acre by 9.354 to return to L/ha. This conversion assumes US liquid gallons.

Why is my fuel use higher than typical values?

Common causes: wet or heavy soil, poorly maintained engine, incorrect ballast/tire pressure, over-sized tractor, field terrain, and low gear/high RPM operation. Compare your per-hour and per-ha figures to extension benchmarks before blaming the tractor.
