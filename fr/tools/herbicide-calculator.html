Questions Fréquemment Posées

How do I calculate the correct Herbicide rate?

To calculate the correct Herbicide rate: (1) Read the product label for the recommended rate per unit area (e.g., L/ha or oz/acre), (2) Measure the area to be treated accurately, (3) Determine your sprayer output through calibration (L/ha or gal/acre), (4) Calculate chemical needed = label rate × area, (5) Calculate water volume = sprayer output × area. Always follow label rates — underdosing leads to poor control and resistance, overdosing wastes money and may cause crop injury or illegal residues.

What personal protective equipment is needed for Herbicide?

Personal protective equipment (PPE) for Herbicide application includes: chemical-resistant gloves (nitrile or neoprene), protective eyewear (goggles or face shield), respirator (N95 or organic vapor cartridge for dusts/sprays), long-sleeved shirt and long pants, chemical-resistant apron, and closed-toe boots. Some products require a full-face respirator or chemical-resistant suit. Always check the label's PPE requirements. Wash PPE separately from regular laundry and shower after application. Never apply pesticides without proper protection.

How do I prevent pesticide resistance from Herbicide?

Prevent resistance from Herbicide by: rotating products with different modes of action (HRAC/WSSA groups), tank-mixing products with different modes of action, applying full labeled rates (never cut rates), integrating non-chemical control methods (crop rotation, cover crops, mechanical weeding, biological control), monitoring for resistant populations, and following resistance management guidelines on the label. Resistance develops when the same mode of action is used repeatedly — a diverse management program is the best defense. Once resistance develops, it is essentially permanent.

What is the pre-harvest interval for Herbicide?

The pre-harvest interval (PHI) is the minimum number of days that must pass between the last pesticide application and harvest. PHIs are specified on the product label and vary by crop and pest. For example, many insecticides have PHIs of 1-14 days, while some fungicides may have PHIs of 0-30 days. Always check the label for the specific crop you are treating. Harvesting before the PHI expires can result in illegal pesticide residues that may cause food safety violations, shipment rejection, or regulatory penalties.

How should I clean my sprayer after Herbicide application?

Proper sprayer cleaning after Herbicide application: (1) Rinse the tank three times with clean water, spraying the rinse through the booms, (2) Use a commercial tank cleaner or ammonia solution (1 L ammonia per 100 L water) for difficult residues, (3) Let the cleaning solution circulate for 10-15 minutes, (4) Spray through booms and nozzles, (5) Remove and clean nozzles and screens individually, (6) Rinse again with clean water, (7) Dispose of rinse water according to label instructions (never dump near water sources). Proper cleaning prevents cross-contamination between pesticides and crops, which can cause severe crop injury.
