Harvest Date Calculator (GDD)

Predict harvest timing from Growing Degree Days accumulation.

Choose your crop, enter the planting date, relative maturity and expected average daily temperature. The calculator applies each crop's base temperature (corn/soybean 50°F, wheat 32°F), totals required GDD, and projects the harvest date.

How the Calculation Works

Daily GDD = (Tmax + Tmin) ÷ 2 − Base temperature. Base temperatures: corn and soybean 50°F (10°C), wheat 32°F (0°C). Required GDD ≈ relative maturity × crop factor (corn ≈ 25, soybean ≈ 26, wheat ≈ 20). Days to harvest = Required GDD ÷ Daily GDD. Harvest date = planting date + days to harvest.

Real-World Example

Corn, planted April 20, 110 RM, average 70°F: base 50°F, daily GDD = 20, required GDD ≈ 2,750. Days = 2,750 ÷ 20 = 138 days. Estimated harvest ≈ September 5.

Common Mistakes & Tips

Common mistakes: using a single year's average temperature; forgetting that GDD stops above the upper cutoff (≈86°F); applying corn factors to wheat; ignoring rainfall and stress that delay maturity. Use long-term average temperatures for planning.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is GDD?

Growing Degree Days (GDD) measure heat accumulation above a crop's base temperature. Each day's GDD = average temperature − base. Crops develop only when GDD accumulate; maturity is reached after a species- and variety-specific total.

What are the base temperatures?

Corn and soybeans use a 50°F (10°C) base; winter wheat uses 32°F (0°C) for spring green-up. Daily GDD below the base is zero, not negative.

How accurate is the harvest date?

It is a planning estimate. Actual maturity shifts with heat, moisture, pests and variety. Use long-term average temperatures and treat the date as ±7 days.

Should I use average daily temperature or Tmax/Tmin?

Best practice uses (Tmax + Tmin)/2. If you only have the daily average, that is an acceptable approximation. Tmax above 86°F is typically capped (86/50 rule for corn).

How does this help with planning?

It lets you schedule custom combines, drying capacity, labor and storage ahead of time, and compare how early vs late planting affects harvest timing across your fields.
